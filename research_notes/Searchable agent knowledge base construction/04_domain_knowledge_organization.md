# Domain Knowledge Organization for Agent-Searchable Knowledge Bases: taxonomies, glossaries, concept graphs, LLM-compiled wikis, Q&A layers (state as of Nov 2026)

Scope: methods that sit above raw chunks and turn a corpus into organized domain knowledge (topics, concepts, definitions, relations, articles, FAQs) that an agent can browse or use to find related topics. Chunk-level indexing, SOP/rule extraction, agentic search loops and memory, lifecycle and products belong to other tracks. GraphRAG-variant benchmarks (GraphRAG-Bench token and accuracy tables, LightRAG, HippoRAG 2 costs) are already in `research_notes/Agent ready data lake toolkit/graph_information_engineering.md`. This file refers to them and does not repeat them.

Evidence labels: **(AR)** author-reported in the paper that proposes the method; **(I)** independent evaluation by a different group; **(V)** vendor or project blog or press; **(S)** secondary or press coverage. Dates are first arXiv or public release. Anything from before 2025 is marked with its year.

## 1. Taxonomy and topic structure: LLM taxonomy construction and expansion, topic modeling, clustering-based navigation, SKOS, facets

### Takeaway
LLMs can now induce usable topic hierarchies from a corpus cheaply. The workable recipe is embed, cluster, have an LLM name and describe each cluster, then build the hierarchy recursively. Clio (Anthropic), TnT-LLM (Microsoft) and Corpus2Skill's compiler all follow it. LLM-only taxonomies are unreliable on specialized domains and at leaf level, so the hierarchy must be grounded in the corpus and validated. The best recent evidence that a topic hierarchy helps agents is Corpus2Skill (EMNLP 2026 Findings). On an 11-dataset study it won on 5, tied on 3 and lost on 3. It helped on single-domain corpora with a recoverable topical taxonomy and lost on open-domain, tabular or long-extractive corpora. The human-search evidence for facets is old (2003) but strong.

### Cited Findings

**Taxonomy construction and expansion methods (method cards)**
- **TaxoLLaMA** (Skoltech/AIRI group; ACL 2024; Mar 2024). LLaMA-2-7B is fine-tuned on WordNet with 4-bit quantization and LoRA, giving one lightweight model for hypernym discovery, taxonomy enrichment, taxonomy construction and lexical entailment. It reports 11 SOTA and 4 top-2 results out of 16 tasks and strong zero-shot results on taxonomy construction (AR). Code and model are open. Cost: one 7B LoRA model, cheap to run locally. Failure mode: the training signal is WordNet's general lexical hierarchy, and domain adaptation needs extra tuning or few-shot examples (AR). — [arXiv 2403.09207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09207)
- **Chain-of-Layer** (Zeng et al., Notre Dame/UW; CIKM 2024; Feb 2024). An in-context method that builds a taxonomy top-down, one layer at a time, from a given entity set. An "Ensemble-based Ranking Filter" cuts hallucinated nodes at each iteration. It reports SOTA on four benchmarks (AR). Cost: a handful of LLM calls per layer. Failure mode: the entity set must already be given, so it does not discover terms. — [arXiv 2402.07386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07386)
- **TaxoInstruct** (Shen, Zhang, Zhang, Han; UIUC; Feb 2024). Unifies entity-set expansion, taxonomy expansion and seed-guided taxonomy construction into two learned skills, "find siblings" and "find parents", via taxonomy-guided instruction tuning. It beats task-specific baselines on all three tasks (AR). It is useful for inserting new concepts into an existing enterprise taxonomy as the corpus grows. — [arXiv 2402.13405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13405)
- **TaxoAdapt** (Kargupta et al., UIUC; ACL 2025; Jun 2025). Adapts an LLM-generated taxonomy to a specific corpus along multiple dimensions (e.g., method, task, dataset, metric) by iterative hierarchical classification. It grows width and depth according to the corpus's topical distribution. It reports taxonomies 26.51% more granularity-preserving and 50.41% more coherent than the most competitive baselines, judged by LLMs (AR). Its key design point is that one document can sit in several facet trees. — [arXiv 2506.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10737)
- **Multi-aspect taxonomy generation for papers** (Zhu et al., HIT/NUS; EMNLP 2025). An LLM extracts aspects per paper (methodology, dataset, evaluation) and writes aspect-specific summaries, which are embedded and clustered per aspect into a hierarchy. It releases a benchmark of 156 expert-crafted taxonomies covering 11.6k papers and reports SOTA coherence, granularity and interpretability (AR). — [arXiv 2509.19125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19125)
- **HiGTL** (Hu et al.; Oct 2024). Builds a taxonomy tree from a citation graph through hierarchical clustering on text plus citation structure, with LLM-generated node names, jointly optimized. Shows that link structure (citations, hyperlinks) can drive clustering alongside text (AR). — [arXiv 2410.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03761)
- **ReLTEx** (Ghamlouch and Alam; Aug 2026). Pairs LLM candidate generation with structure-aware validation and recursive expansion control. Motivation: raw LLM expansions are "noisy, redundant, or hierarchically inconsistent". It reports more reliable expansions under masked-taxonomy evaluation and human evaluation (AR). — [arXiv 2608.10970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10970)
- **TaxMorph** (Golde et al.; Jan 2026). An LLM rewrites a whole existing taxonomy by renaming, merging, splitting and reordering. LLM-refined taxonomies beat human-curated ones by up to +2.9 F1 points on three hierarchical text-classification benchmarks. Human taxonomies form more separable embedding clusters, but the LLM-refined ones match model confusion patterns better (AR). The practical implication is to let an LLM "lint" a legacy taxonomy before using it to route agents. — [arXiv 2601.18375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18375)
- **LLM-driven ontology learning.** LLMs4OL (ISWC 2023) tested 9 LLM families zero-shot on term typing, taxonomy discovery and non-taxonomic relation extraction over WordNet, GeoNames and UMLS. It became a recurring challenge, with LLMs4OL 2024 and 2026 shared tasks. — [arXiv 2307.16648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16648); [LLMs4OL 2024 overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10146). OLLM (Cambridge; Oct 2024) fine-tunes an LLM to generate whole ontology subgraphs from scratch on Wikipedia and beats subtask-composition methods on new graph-similarity metrics (AR). — [arXiv 2410.23584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23584)
- **Limits of LLM parametric taxonomies.** TaxoGlimpse (Meta/HKUST; VLDB 2024) tested 18 LLMs on 10 taxonomies from common to specialized domains. It found that LLMs "can still not well capture the knowledge of specialized taxonomies and leaf-level entities" (I, a benchmark study rather than a method paper). — [arXiv 2406.11131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11131)
- **Enterprise ontology from documents.** OntoEKG (ICSC 2026) runs class and property extraction, then an entailment step that builds the hierarchy and serializes it to RDF. It reached fuzzy-match F1 0.724 in the Data domain and showed weaknesses in "scope definition and hierarchical reasoning" (AR). — [arXiv 2602.01276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01276)

**Topic modeling and clustering-based structure**
- **BERTopic** (Grootendorst; 2022). Embeds documents with a transformer, reduces dimensionality and clusters (UMAP and HDBSCAN in the reference implementation), then labels each cluster with class-based TF-IDF. It is competitive with classical and clustering topic models (AR). It is the de facto open-source baseline. — [arXiv 2203.05794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05794)
- **TopicGPT** (Pham et al., UMass/UMD; NAACL 2024; Nov 2023). Has an LLM generate topics with natural-language labels and descriptions from a document sample, merges near-duplicates (cosine ≥ 0.5), then assigns topics to every document with a supporting quote. A hierarchical extension produces subtopics. On Wikipedia it scores harmonic-mean purity 0.74, ARI 0.60 and NMI 0.70, against LDA 0.64/0.52/0.67 and BERTopic 0.58/0.28/0.50. Human-judged misalignment falls from 62.4% (LDA) to 30.3% (AR). Estimated cost is about $88 on Bills (GPT-4 to generate, GPT-3.5 to assign) and about $155 on Wiki. Failure modes: open models (Mistral-7B) cannot reliably generate topics (1,418 topics produced); refinement can drop real categories ("Culture" was lost); outputs are non-deterministic; the Wiki labels may have been memorized in pretraining. — [arXiv 2311.01449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01449)
- **LLM topic extraction as an alternative to LDA** (Mu et al., Sheffield; LREC-COLING 2024). With suitable prompts LLMs produce relevant topic titles and follow guidelines to refine and merge topics. It also documents constraints. — [arXiv 2403.16248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16248)
- **TnT-LLM** (Microsoft; Mar 2024). Phase 1: zero-shot, multi-stage LLM reasoning iteratively produces and refines a label taxonomy. Phase 2: the LLM pseudo-labels data to train lightweight classifiers that run at scale. Applied to Bing Copilot intent and domain analysis, it produced more accurate and relevant taxonomies than baselines (AR). Its main lesson is the cost pattern: use the LLM to design the taxonomy and label a sample, then use cheap classifiers to tag the full corpus. — [arXiv 2403.12173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12173)
- **Clio** (Anthropic; Dec 2024). Extracts facets per conversation, embeds one facet, clusters with k-means, has an LLM title and summarize each cluster, then recursively builds a multi-level hierarchy. The top level has a few dozen patterns and lower levels have thousands of categories. On 19,476 synthetic transcripts with known categories it recovered ground truth at 94% accuracy, against 5% random. It estimates $48.81 per 100k-conversation run (AR). — [arXiv 2412.13678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678)
- **LLooM concept induction** (Stanford/UW; CHI 2024). An LLM iteratively synthesizes sampled text into high-level concepts, each with explicit inclusion criteria. Where BERTopic gives "women, power, female", LLooM gives "Criticism of traditional gender roles". It reports better quality and coverage than topic models (AR). — [arXiv 2404.12259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12259)
- **Evaluating topic and cluster quality.** ProxAnn (ACL 2025) is a use-oriented protocol: annotators infer a category from a cluster's items and then apply it to new documents. The best LLM proxies were "statistically indistinguishable from a human annotator" (I relative to the topic models evaluated). It can serve as a cheap QA gate for auto-generated topic nodes. — [arXiv 2507.00828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00828)

**Evidence that hierarchy/taxonomy/facets improve retrieval or navigation**
- **Corpus2Skill** (Sun, Wei, Hsieh; EMNLP 2026 Findings; Apr 2026). The offline compiler builds an LLM summary card per document, embeds it (Qwen3-Embedding-8B), runs bottom-up k-means with a branching ratio p, has an LLM (Claude Sonnet 4.6) summarize each cluster, and runs verify-and-repartition. It adds soft assignment of borderline documents and an entity index with "Related skills" cross-links. The output is a forest of SKILL.md/INDEX.md files, and the agent navigates it with ls/cat/grep plus `get_document`, with no vector DB at serve time. Results on WixQA (6,221 articles, 200 queries), token F1 / factuality / $ per query: Dense 0.364 / 0.537 / $0.007; RAPTOR 0.398 / 0.704 / $0.012; agentic RAG 0.378 / 0.705 / $0.082; Corpus2Skill 0.456 / 0.767 / $0.153. Its hallucination rate is 4.5% against 0% for dense, because single-shot retrievers feed one small passage. Compile cost is $3.33 for 6k docs and $54.1 for 50k. On the 11 datasets it wins on WixQA, Emanual, DelucionQA, ExpertQA and ClapNQ, ties on TechQA, CovidQA and FinQA, and loses on HAGRID, TatQA and CUAD. Failure modes: most errors are top-level routing mistakes; wide trees (p=20) degrade sharply; any corpus churn requires recompilation (AR). — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572)
- **Taxonomy-routed product search.** CoT tree-search query categorization into e-commerce leaf categories beat embedding-based category prediction on human-judged pairs (SIGIR eCom 2025, AR, industry). — [arXiv 2601.00510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00510). BEATS (SIGIR 2026 Industry) bootstraps product attribute taxonomies with LLMs plus human-in-the-loop validation. It argues that missing attribute schemas prevent faceted filtering and degrade query understanding (AR). — [arXiv 2606.04909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04909)
- **Facets in human search (2003).** In the Flamenco study, 32 art-history students explored 35,000 images. Against a keyword baseline, 90% preferred hierarchical faceted metadata, 97% said it helped them learn about the collection, 75% found it more flexible and 72% found it easier to use (Yee, Swearingen, Li, Hearst, CHI 2003). — [Sciweavers abstract of CHI 2003 paper](https://sciweavers.org/node/46167)
- **SKOS (W3C Recommendation, 18 Aug 2009)** is the standard interchange model for thesauri and taxonomies. It defines `skos:Concept` and `ConceptScheme`; labels `prefLabel`, `altLabel` and `hiddenLabel`; relations `broader`, `narrower` and `related`; documentation `definition` and `scopeNote`; and mappings `exactMatch` (transitive) and `closeMatch`. It allows at most one `prefLabel` per language tag. — [W3C SKOS Reference](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/). LLMs are already used to translate SKOS thesauri for multilingual access (digital humanities, 2025). — [arXiv 2507.19537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19537)

### Inferences
- The default taxonomy pipeline for the toolkit should follow the Clio/TnT-LLM/Corpus2Skill pattern: embeddings, recursive k-means with a moderate branching factor (Corpus2Skill shows p=20 hurts), an LLM name, description and inclusion criterion per node, then a verify/repartition pass. Store the result as SKOS (prefLabel/altLabel/broader/related/definition) plus markdown INDEX pages. This makes it both standard and agent-browsable.
- TaxoGlimpse and the OntoEKG results argue against letting an LLM invent the hierarchy from parametric knowledge. Ground it in corpus clusters and let the LLM only name, merge and lint (TaxMorph-style).
- Multi-facet trees (TaxoAdapt, multi-aspect clustering) fit heterogeneous enterprise data better than one tree: e.g., product, process, document type, regulation, org unit. Documents carry several facet assignments, which also act as metadata filters for search.
- Corpus2Skill gives a concrete gating rule. Enable navigation only when the corpus has a recoverable topical structure, for example when top-level clusters pass a ProxAnn-style coherence check. Otherwise fall back to flat hybrid retrieval.

### Gaps
- No independent replication of Corpus2Skill was found; all its numbers are author-reported.
- I found no 2024–2026 controlled study measuring whether facets or taxonomies improve LLM-agent retrieval on enterprise corpora specifically (as opposed to e-commerce search or human browsing). The facet evidence for humans dates from 2003.
- The full text of Yee et al. 2003 could not be retrieved (403). The figures come from the abstract on Sciweavers.

## 2. Glossaries and concept extraction: terms, definitions, business glossaries, acronyms, synonym/alias tables, entity-centric pages

### Takeaway
Term and definition extraction is far from solved. On scientific papers the best LLM configuration scores only 0.397 set-level, and high recall comes with over-generation. Acronym disambiguation is mature when a domain dictionary exists. The highest-value glossary artifact for retrieval is an alias/synonym/acronym table that feeds query expansion and metadata boosting: UMLS-guided expansion gave up to +22.1% NDCG@10 over sparse baselines, and MetaGen's concept/topic/acronym metadata improved zero-shot enterprise RAG. Entity-centric pages are the natural unit for both the glossary and the wiki layer (Section 3).

### Cited Findings
- **Definition extraction with LLMs.** SciDef (CIKM 2026 resources, under review; Feb 2026) contributes DefExtra (268 human-validated definitions from 75 papers) and DefSim. Across 16 LLMs the best set-level score was 0.397. The highest-coverage configuration matched 86.4% of gold definitions but over-generated, and relevance-aware filtering is identified as the bottleneck. An NLI-based match metric agreed strongly with humans (AR). — [arXiv 2602.05413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05413)
- **Statutory definitions (law).** A Legal-BERT pipeline over the XML U.S. Code (more than 200k pages) classifies definitional paragraphs, aggregates them into definitional units, and extracts the defined term plus its jurisdictional scope (e.g., "for purposes of this chapter") (IEEE AIIoT 2025, AR). Scope is the key extra field a legal glossary needs. — [arXiv 2504.16353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16353)
- **Term/keyword extraction with LLMs** (2024). GPT-3.5 reached Jaccard 0.64 on Inspec but only 0.21 on PubMed, with Llama2-7B and Falcon-7B lower. Hallucinated keywords complicate evaluation (AR). — [arXiv 2404.02330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02330). Retrieval of syntactically similar demonstrations improves LLM term extraction (2025, AR). — [arXiv 2506.21222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21222). Distant supervision supports term extraction across domains without labels (2025, AR). — [arXiv 2510.06838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06838)
- **Acronyms.** GLADIS (EACL 2023) built a dictionary of 1.5M acronyms and 6.4M long forms, a 160M-sentence pretraining corpus and AcroBERT. — [arXiv 2302.01860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01860). DACE (Dec 2025) won the TextMine'26 French railway acronym-disambiguation task with F1 0.9069 using dynamic prompting, RAG over domain knowledge and ensembling (AR). — [arXiv 2512.18357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18357)
- **Acronyms and jargon as an enterprise retrieval failure.** A production telecom system (DocuSearch, 2026) says dense retrieval "frequently performs poorly on queries that mix technical terminology, vendor-specific acronyms". It fuses vector (weight 0.50), BM25 (0.35) and KG-neighbour expansion (0.15) via RRF (AR, single deployment). — [arXiv 2609.01617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01617)
- **Glossary/metadata-enriched indexing.** MetaGen Blended RAG (IBM authors; May 2025) generates key concepts, topics and acronyms as metadata, builds hybrid dense+sparse indexes and boosts queries with them. It reports 82% retrieval accuracy and 77% RAG accuracy zero-shot on PubMedQA (AR). — [arXiv 2505.18247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18247)
- **Ontology/thesaurus-guided query expansion.** BMQExpander (Aug 2025) combines UMLS definitions and relations with LLM generation. On NFCorpus, TREC-COVID and SciFact it reports up to +22.1% NDCG@10 over sparse baselines and +6.5% over the strongest baseline, is more robust under query paraphrase (+15.7%), and hallucinates less than LLM-only expansion (AR). — [arXiv 2508.11784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11784). A 2025 survey of QE in the LLM era names KG-grounded expansion as one of four design axes and stresses the trade-off between grounding and cost. — [arXiv 2509.07794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07794). KAR (Adobe/UCSD; NAACL 2025) adds KG document relations to LLM query expansion for semi-structured queries (AR). — [arXiv 2410.13765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13765)
- **Business glossaries.** IBM Research (2023, work in progress, "limited evidence") used LLMs to match table column metadata to business-glossary entries without manual tuning, so a glossary can be used for discovery before data access is granted. — [arXiv 2309.11506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11506)
- **Clinical concept sets.** CUICurate (JAMIA 2026) embeds a UMLS KG, expands candidate CUIs through the graph, then filters with GPT-5 or Qwen3-32B. GPT-5 beat manual curation on all five concepts and kept at least 95% of definitive gold CUIs (AR). It shows the pattern synonym/subtype graph expansion → LLM filter → curated alias set. — [arXiv 2602.17949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17949)
- **Concept-card representation.** "Knowledge Cards" (Jul 2026) propose a per-concept artifact recording the entities, relationships, reasoning, the conditions under which the reasoning no longer holds, and provenance for every claim. Cards are grounded in a formal ontology and signed off by a domain expert. This is a position paper with no empirical evaluation. — [arXiv 2608.26176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26176)

### Inferences
- The glossary layer should be structured: SKOS Concept, prefLabel, altLabels (synonyms, acronyms, misspellings, legacy product names), definition with source span, scope (from legal-definition work), broader/related, and owner/approval status (from Knowledge Cards). The same table drives (a) query expansion and metadata boosting at search time, (b) entity linking for graph and wiki pages, and (c) a `define(term)` agent tool.
- Given SciDef's over-generation finding, auto-extracted definitions should be stored as candidates with source spans. Promote them only after NLI or LLM verification against the span, plus optional human sign-off for regulated domains.
- Acronym expansion should be domain-scoped: one acronym maps to several long forms keyed by facet or department, with context-based disambiguation. GLADIS-scale generic dictionaries are a fallback, not the primary source.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark measures end-to-end agent QA gains from an auto-built enterprise glossary or alias table. The evidence is per-component (QE on biomedical IR, metadata on PubMedQA).
- No independent evaluation of commercial "business glossary + AI" products (Atlan, Collibra and others) was found. I did not survey them here; they belong to the products track.

## 3. Synthesized knowledge layers: LLM-generated wikis and articles (STORM, Co-STORM, OmniThink, WikiAutoGen, DeepWiki/code wikis, the "LLM wiki" or "knowledge compilation" pattern), community reports, FAQ/Q&A generation, knowledge cards; faithfulness

### Takeaway
Since April 2026 the field has converged on "knowledge compilation": an LLM maintains interlinked markdown pages (entity/concept pages, summaries, indexes, logs) between raw sources and the agent. Karpathy's LLM Wiki gist popularized it, and academic versions followed (LLM-Wiki, WikiLoop, ASKS, RAGFlow's compilation layer). Author-reported multi-hop QA gains over HippoRAG 2, LightRAG and GraphRAG are 2–12 F1 points. The strongest counter-evidence also dates from 2026: under a competent agentic search harness, compiled wikis, graphs and summary trees gave no detectable accuracy gain on FRAMES (RAGFlow, vendor-run). Their measurable value was cutting context tokens, by 38% on average, when compiled as claim+verbatim-evidence leaves. Faithfulness is the main risk. STORM had about 15% unsupported sentences, PaperQA2's cited summaries were more accurate than human Wikipedia, and generated Q&A or doc2query layers need filtering (Doc2Query-- filtering gave up to +16% effectiveness and -33% index size).

### Cited Findings

**LLM-wiki / knowledge compilation pattern**
- **Karpathy "LLM Wiki" gist** (Apr 4, 2026; after an X post on Apr 2–3). It has three layers: immutable raw sources; an LLM-owned markdown wiki (summaries, entity and concept pages, comparisons, overview); and a schema file (CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md) that makes the LLM "a disciplined wiki maintainer". `index.md` is a categorized catalog with one-line summaries that the LLM reads first; `log.md` is append-only and grep-able. Operations are **ingest** ("a single source might touch 10-15 wiki pages"), **query** (answers can be filed back as pages) and **lint** (contradictions, stale claims, orphan pages, missing concept pages and cross-references). It claims the index approach "works surprisingly well at moderate scale (~100 sources, ~hundreds of pages)". Search tooling becomes useful as the wiki grows; it suggests qmd (hybrid BM25/vector plus LLM rerank, local, with an MCP server) (V, opinion/design). — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f); [qmd repo (MIT)](https://github.com/tobi/qmd)
- Press coverage reports Karpathy's own wiki at about 100 articles and 400k words, navigated "without retrieval infrastructure". It also reports critiques: scaling to enterprises with "contradictory records" is hard (Eugen Alpeza); Steph Ango (Obsidian) suggests keeping the agent's "messy vault" separate from a clean personal vault; and hallucinations can propagate in multi-agent setups, so a quality gate is suggested (S). — [VentureBeat, Apr 3 2026](https://venturebeat.com/data/karpathy-shares-llm-knowledge-base-architecture-that-bypasses-rag-with-an)
- **LLM-Wiki (agent-native retrieval)** (Ming, Li, Wu, Que; May 2026). Each passage is compiled against the current wiki: select existing pages, then generate or update knowledge pages (people, orgs, concepts and others) with YAML frontmatter, summary, key facts, bidirectional wikilinks and source references, plus directory `_index.md` pages and source-record pages. Tools are `wiki_search` (ranks names, aliases, tags and descriptions before content) and `wiki_read`, and the agent follows links. An "Error Book" logs compile errors (dangling links were 55.8–63.8% of errors) and injects them as rules into later compilation. F1 on HotpotQA / MuSiQue / 2Wiki: LLM-Wiki 0.839 / 0.739 / 0.911; LightRAG 0.819 / 0.659 / 0.847; HippoRAG 2 0.805 / 0.624 / 0.831; GraphRAG 0.771 / 0.582 / 0.720; dense 0.764 / 0.611 / 0.815. Ablations: no wiki structure -6.1 to -7.0; no progressive traversal -11.7 to -13.8; no Error Book -3.4 to -4.0. Query latency is about that of dense RAG (14.9 s vs 16.3 s on HotpotQA) and well below LightRAG (41.4 s). Indexing cost is not reported, only "more expensive than chunk-and-embed". Failure modes: directory indices get unwieldy at tens of thousands of pages; structured pages omit fine local detail (HippoRAG 2 beat it on single-doc AuthTrace, 78.3 vs 76.0); evaluated only on dataset-provided paragraphs (AR). — [arXiv 2605.25480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480)
- **WikiLoop** (same group; Jul 2026). Jointly trains a Builder, which proposes wiki edits scored by the change in downstream navigation performance with a guard against regressions, and a Navigator. On Qwen3.5-9B it reaches 62.6 aggregate Answer Correctness on AuthTrace, +6.3 over LLM-Wiki base. Learned edits transfer to a held-out Navigator (AR). It suggests compile quality can be optimized from query logs. — [arXiv 2607.26604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26604)
- **WFM, Wiki Foundation Model** (Tencent-affiliated authors; Sep 2026). Claims "the entire industry is witnessing a paradigm shift from traditional sparse graphs to LLM Wiki". Proposes a Wiki Graph schema with query-conditioned message passing for retrieval over wiki pages and reports 10.5× training acceleration (AR). — [arXiv 2609.18182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18182)
- **ASKS, scientific knowledge compilation** (Aug 2026). Per source, an LLM produces a readable wiki view plus machine-facing semantics. Deterministic checks turn the semantics into a document-local "GraphDelta", which is merged via embedding geometry and explicit graph rules. Every ingest is an inspectable state transition linked to the source record. On 56 papers compiled chronologically, high-level hub organization stayed stable and low-churn, and canonical-node growth was mostly additive (AR, case study). — [arXiv 2608.29612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29612)
- **llm-wiki-memory-template** (May 2026). An append-only, agent-aware wiki template for multi-human and multi-agent work that preserves failure paths. One retroactive audit revised two experiments' claimed 20/20 coverage down to 14 and 12 (AR, case studies). — [arXiv 2607.24759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24759). A governance profile for single-user LLM wikis targets "entrenchment" (contradicting evidence suppressed by centrality-protected pages) with TRIAGE, DECAY, CONTEXTUALIZE, CONSOLIDATE and AUDIT operations (position paper). — [arXiv 2604.12034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12034)
- **Counter-evidence: "Context as Infrastructure"** (InfiniFlow/RAGFlow + ECNU; Oct 30 2026; vendor). With a BM25/grep-only harness and MiniMax M3, the uncompiled baseline reached 91.0% on FRAMES (about 324k tokens per question, 46.7 s) and 85.0% on BrowseComp-Plus. RAPTOR summaries, wiki and graph artifacts were accuracy-neutral. A PageIndex-style outline was neutral at small scale and negative at FRAMES scale. A navigation tree whose leaves are claim + verbatim-evidence pairs cut query tokens by 91% when evidence sufficed and added 1.9% when it did not, a 38% average reduction with about 2% break-even sufficiency. On a 7-question subset, latency roughly tripled even as tokens halved. Single runs, no confidence intervals, no measured compile cost. Recommendations: "compile evidence rather than prose", keep verbatim pointers, and check finite-horizon break-even before compiling (V). — [arXiv 2610.41172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172)
- **Navigation without LLM summaries.** NavTree (Sep 2026) builds a deterministic segment tree over chunks with zero LLM calls and walks it with a hybrid frontier, emitting only leaves. On matched-cost evaluation it beat an extractive RAPTOR re-implementation and the published abstractive RAPTOR at every multi-chunk budget, and tied the best flat baseline. Conclusion: "the main benefit of summary trees ... can come from navigation rather than the generated summary content" (I with respect to RAPTOR). — [arXiv 2610.06902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06902)

**Article and wiki generation systems (method cards)**
- **STORM** (Stanford OVAL; NAACL 2024; Feb 2024). Discovers perspectives from related topics' Wikipedia tables of contents, simulates multi-perspective writer–expert conversations grounded in search, drafts and refines an outline, then writes sections with citations. Against an outline-RAG baseline: heading soft recall 92.73% vs 89.55%; entity recall 14.10 vs 12.57; citation recall 84.83% and precision 85.18%. Editors rated organization ≥4 on 70% of STORM articles vs 45% for the baseline. About 15% of sentences were unsupported, mostly through "improper inferential linking". Editors also flagged red-herring fallacy, overspeculation, and bias or tone transferred from sources. 7 of 10 editors found the output "emotional" or "unneutral". Cost is not reported (AR). — [arXiv 2402.14207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207)
- **Co-STORM** (Stanford; EMNLP 2024; Aug 2024). Multiple LM agents converse while the user observes or steers. Discovered information is organized into a dynamic mind map, then a report. 70% of participants preferred it to a search engine and 78% to a RAG chatbot (AR). The mind map is a candidate structure for a "related topics" view. — [arXiv 2408.15232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15232)
- **OmniThink** (Zhejiang U./Alibaba; EMNLP 2025; Jan 2025). "Slow-thinking" iterative expansion and reflection builds an information tree and conceptual pool before writing. It raises the knowledge density of articles without hurting coherence (AR). Code open. — [arXiv 2501.09751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09751)
- **WikiAutoGen** (KAUST et al.; ICCV 2025; Mar 2025). Multimodal Wikipedia-style generation that retrieves images alongside text, with multi-perspective self-reflection. Reports +8–29% over prior methods on its WikiSeek benchmark (AR). — [arXiv 2503.19065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19065)
- **WikiGenBench** (COLING 2025; Feb 2024) has 1,320 entries for full-length cited Wikipedia generation on new events. Hierarchical/outline methods gave more comprehensive content and fine-tuned methods better verifiability, but "even the best methods still show a significant gap compared to existing Wikipedia content" (I, benchmark). — [arXiv 2402.18264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18264)
- **PaperQA2 / WikiCrow** (FutureHouse; Sep 2024). An agent writes cited, Wikipedia-style summaries of scientific topics (genes). Experts judged them "significantly more accurate than existing, human-written Wikipedia articles". It also finds contradictions: 2.34 ± 1.99 per paper, 70% validated by experts (AR, with expert human evaluation). — [arXiv 2409.13740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13740)
- **Code wikis.** DeepWiki (Cognition; launched May 5 2025 per Cognition's post, late April per third parties) is the free public version of Devin Wiki/Search. It had indexed more than 50,000 top public GitHub repos at launch. Cognition's post makes no accuracy claims (V). — [Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/deepwiki). Google Code Wiki (public preview Nov 2025) regenerates a repo's structured wiki after code changes, keeps links to every symbol, auto-generates architecture, class and sequence diagrams, and uses the wiki as the knowledge base for a Gemini chat agent. Coverage includes user scepticism that the agent could read the code directly (S). — [InfoQ, Nov 21 2025](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/11/google-code-wiki/). CodeWiki (ACL 2026) uses hierarchical decomposition and recursive multi-agent generation. On CodeWikiBench it scores 68.79% quality vs DeepWiki 64.06% under LLM-rubric judging (AR, with DeepWiki as a closed baseline). — [arXiv 2510.24428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24428)
- **Scholarly synthesis systems.** Ai2 Scholar QA (ACL 2025 demo) is an open pipeline producing organized, sectioned literature syntheses with attribution, and reports beating competing systems on a recent scientific QA benchmark (AR). — [arXiv 2504.10861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10861). OpenScholar (Ai2/UW; Nov 2024) is a retrieval-augmented LM for synthesizing scientific literature with citations. — [arXiv 2411.14199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14199)

**Community/summary reports (GraphRAG global) and summary trees**
- **GraphRAG** (Microsoft Research; Apr 2024). LLM entity, relation and claim extraction per 600-token chunk with "gleaning"; Leiden hierarchy C0–C3; an LLM-written community report per community (title, summary, impact rating, findings citing records); map-reduce global answers. Global modes beat vector RAG on comprehensiveness (72–83%) and diversity (62–82%) in LLM-judged head-to-heads on about 1M-token corpora. Root-level C0 used 2.3–2.6% of the tokens of map-reduce over source text. Fabrication rates were not measured (AR). — [arXiv 2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130). The repo warns "indexing can be an expensive operation" and says the project "is largely in maintenance mode" (V; the date of the maintenance notice was not visible). — [microsoft/graphrag](https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag)
- **Evaluation bias.** An unbiased re-evaluation (May 2025) found that GraphRAG-paper questions are vague and LLM judges have position, length and trial biases. LightRAG's reported 66.70% win rate over naive RAG fell to 39.06%, with NaiveRAG slightly ahead (I). — [arXiv 2506.06331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06331)
- **LazyGraphRAG** (Microsoft Research blog, Nov 25 2024). Builds a concept graph from NLP noun-phrase co-occurrence with no LLM summarization at index time; indexing costs the same as vector RAG and 0.1% of GraphRAG. Query time combines best-first and breadth-first relevance testing under a budget. It claims quality comparable to GraphRAG global at more than 700× lower query cost. Integrated into Microsoft Discovery and Azure Local preview (June 2025 note) (V). — [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)
- **RAPTOR** (Stanford; ICLR 2024; Jan 2024). Recursively embeds, clusters (soft clustering) and summarizes chunks into a tree and retrieves across levels. +20% absolute on QuALITY with GPT-4 (AR). — [arXiv 2401.18059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059). Independent results are mixed: weaker on fact and reasoning in GraphRAG-Bench (see the earlier notes file); beaten by the zero-LLM NavTree; accuracy-neutral in RAGFlow's harness. — [arXiv 2610.06902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06902); [arXiv 2610.41172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172)

**FAQ / Q&A-pair generation as an index layer**
- **doc2query** (Nogueira, Yang, Lin, Cho; 2019). Predicts queries a document answers and appends them before indexing. In a latency-critical setting, retrieval alone approaches neural re-rankers (AR). — [arXiv 1904.08375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375). **Doc2Query--** (Glasgow; ECIR 2023) found that generated queries hallucinate. Filtering them with a relevance model gave up to +16% effectiveness, -23% query time and -33% index size (I with respect to doc2query). — [arXiv 2301.03266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266). **Doc2Query++** (Oct 2025) adds topic-guided LLM query generation and dual-index fusion, which keeps question signals separate from text for dense retrieval (AR). — [arXiv 2510.09557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09557)
- **QuOTE** (Virginia Tech/Vectorize; Feb 2025). Stores generated questions per chunk as separate entries, retrieves k×M, then deduplicates to chunks. With 10 questions: SQuAD context Acc@1 67.11% → 77.58%; MultiHop-RAG Full@20 22.5% → 35.0%. The sweet spot is 10–15 questions, and 30 hurts. Index build took 170 s vs 10 s. Some embedding models got worse (text-embedding-3-small Full@20 32.5% → 27.5%), the paper's tables are internally inconsistent, and a basic prompt hurt MultiHop (AR). — [arXiv 2502.10976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10976)
- **QuIM-RAG** (IEEE Access 2024) matches user questions against questions generated from chunks (question-to-question inverted index) on a custom 500+ page corpus with Llama-3-8B (AR, small study). — [arXiv 2501.02702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02702)

**Faithfulness evaluation tools**
- **FActScore** (UW/Meta/AI2; EMNLP 2023) decomposes long-form text into atomic facts and computes the share supported by a knowledge source. ChatGPT scored only 58% on biographies, and an automated estimator is provided. — [arXiv 2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251). STORM-style citation recall and precision (above) and NLI-based matching (SciDef) are the practical checks for compiled pages.

### Inferences
- The synthesized layer should be **evidence-first pages, not free prose**. Each wiki or entity page holds atomic claims, each with a verbatim source span and doc ID (RAGFlow's claim leaves, ASKS's source-linked GraphDelta, LLM-Wiki's source references). Narrative summaries are a rendering of those claims. This targets STORM's main error (improper inferential linking) and makes FActScore-style verification cheap.
- Treat the wiki as a navigation and context-compression layer, not an accuracy guarantee. Accuracy gains are documented on multi-hop benchmarks with author-run baselines (LLM-Wiki, WikiLoop). Under a strong harness the measurable win is fewer tokens (RAGFlow). Ablations in both LLM-Wiki and NavTree point to *traversal structure* as the lever, more than generated summary text.
- Generated Q&A/FAQ entries are worth adding as a separate, filtered index (Doc2Query-- filtering, QuOTE dedup, Doc2Query++ dual index). Do not concatenate them into chunk text, and cap the count at about 10–15 per chunk.
- The Karpathy pattern's index.md + log.md + lint loop maps directly to toolkit features: an auto-maintained catalog, an append-only change log for provenance, and a scheduled lint job (orphans, dangling links, contradictions, stale claims). LLM-Wiki's Error Book shows lint findings can feed back into compilation prompts.
- Scale limits are consistent across sources. Index-only navigation is claimed to work to "~hundreds of pages" (Karpathy). LLM-Wiki flags tens of thousands of pages as a problem, and Corpus2Skill needs recompilation on churn. At data-lake scale the wiki needs hybrid search over pages (qmd-style) plus sharded, hierarchical indexes.

### Gaps
- No independent, cost-controlled comparison of LLM-wiki compilation vs GraphRAG vs HippoRAG 2 vs flat hybrid retrieval on an enterprise corpus was found. All LLM-Wiki and WikiLoop numbers come from one group, and the RAGFlow counter-evidence is vendor-run with single runs.
- Compile cost of LLM-Wiki is not reported. Corpus2Skill's $3.33/6k docs is the only concrete compile price found for a wiki/skill-style layer (other than Clio's $48.81 per 100k items for clustering and labeling).
- No public faithfulness audit of DeepWiki or Google Code Wiki output was found. CodeWiki's comparison uses LLM rubrics.

## 4. Concept/knowledge graphs for navigation and related-topic discovery (as opposed to exact queries)

### Takeaway
For "find related topics" the useful graph is a navigation graph of concept/entity pages, communities and typed "related" links, walked by the agent or by Personalized PageRank. It is not an exact-query KG. Evidence favors (a) associative spreading (HippoRAG/HippoRAG 2), (b) explicit links between high-level summaries so communities are not "semantic islands" (LeanRAG, ArchRAG), (c) indexing both similarity and relatedness (SiReRAG), and (d) cheap concept graphs (LazyGraphRAG noun-phrase graphs, KET-RAG keyword bipartite graphs, author hyperlinks via LARAG). Independent re-evaluations show many graph-RAG gains are smaller than reported.

### Cited Findings
- **HippoRAG** (OSU; NeurIPS 2024; May 2024). Open-IE KG plus Personalized PageRank seeded from query entities, modeled on hippocampal indexing. Up to +20% on multi-hop QA. Single-step retrieval matched or beat iterative IRCoT while being 10–30× cheaper and 6–13× faster (AR). — [arXiv 2405.14831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831). **HippoRAG 2** (ICML 2025) adds passage nodes and LLM recognition filtering, fixing earlier graph-RAG drops on simple factual tasks. It reports a 7% gain on associative-memory tasks over a SOTA embedder (AR). Independent GraphRAG-Bench results and token costs (about 1.15× vanilla RAG tokens) are in the earlier notes file. — [arXiv 2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)
- **SiReRAG** (Penn State/Salesforce; ICLR 2025). Builds a similarity tree (recursive summaries) and a relatedness tree (propositions grouped by shared entities, then summarized) and flattens both into one pool. +1.9% average F1 over SOTA indexing on MuSiQue, 2Wiki and HotpotQA (AR). — [arXiv 2412.06206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06206)
- **LeanRAG** (AAAI 2026). Identifies "semantic islands": high-level community summaries lack explicit relations. It aggregates entity clusters, creates explicit relations between aggregate summaries so the network is navigable, then retrieves bottom-up from fine entities along those paths (AR). — [arXiv 2508.10391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10391)
- **ArchRAG** (CUHK-SZ/Huawei; AAAI 2026). Attributed communities via LLM-based hierarchical clustering, a hierarchical community index and online retrieval. It reports better accuracy and lower token cost than prior graph RAG (AR). — [arXiv 2502.09891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09891)
- **KET-RAG** (NUS; Feb 2025). Uses an LLM to build a KG skeleton from only a small set of key chunks and a cheap text-keyword bipartite graph for all chunks, cutting indexing cost while keeping graph-RAG quality (AR). — [arXiv 2502.09304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09304). **NodeRAG** (Apr 2025) uses heterogeneous node types (entities, relations, summaries, attributes, text) to integrate graph algorithms into RAG (AR). — [arXiv 2504.11544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11544)
- **Existing link structure.** LARAG (May 2026) encodes author-defined hyperlinks in HTML technical documentation as chunk metadata for graph-like local expansion. On 20 expert queries over Rulex docs it scored the highest BERTScore while retrieving fewer chunks (AR, small study). — [arXiv 2605.07517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07517)
- **Ontology-grounded hypergraphs.** OG-RAG (Microsoft; Dec 2024) clusters facts into hyperedges grounded in a domain ontology and retrieves the minimal hyperedge set covering the query. Targets workflow and rule domains (healthcare, agriculture and similar) (AR). — [arXiv 2412.15235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15235). PolyRAG's "knowledge pyramid" (ontology → KG → raw chunks) retrieves top-down until confident and reports 0.1636 → 0.8109 F1 for GPT-4 on its own academic and finance benchmarks (AR, own benchmarks). — [arXiv 2407.21276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21276)
- **Related-topics in the wiki form.** Corpus2Skill adds an entity index with "Related skills" cross-links. LLM-Wiki uses bidirectional wikilinks, though its ablations do not isolate links. Co-STORM organizes discovered information into a mind map. — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572); [arXiv 2605.25480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480); [arXiv 2408.15232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15232)
- **Concept-graph query expansion.** BMQExpander (UMLS relations), KAR (KG document relations) and DocuSearch's KG-neighbour expansion at weight 0.15 in RRF (Section 2) are the main forms of graph-driven expansion for retrieval (AR). — [arXiv 2508.11784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11784); [arXiv 2410.13765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13765); [arXiv 2609.01617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01617)
- **Caution on evaluation.** Unbiased evaluation cut LightRAG's win rate over naive RAG from 66.70% to 39.06% (I). Microsoft's own GraphRAG paper did not measure fabrication (AR). — [arXiv 2506.06331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06331); [arXiv 2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130)

### Inferences
- The "related topics" feature can be built cheaply without full LLM triple extraction: (1) concept co-occurrence graph from noun phrases or glossary terms (LazyGraphRAG/KET-RAG style); (2) taxonomy siblings and parents; (3) embedding kNN between concept pages; (4) explicit author links (hyperlinks, citations, cross-references); (5) LLM-proposed `skos:related` links, verified against shared evidence. Rank related topics by PPR from the current page or concept (HippoRAG) and expose them as a `related(concept)` tool and as a "Related" section on each page.
- Link communities and summary pages explicitly (LeanRAG's lesson). A taxonomy node page should list its sibling and cross-facet relations, not only its children.
- Reserve full GraphRAG community reports for corpus-level "what are the themes" questions. Given cost, the maintenance-mode status and evaluation-bias findings, they should be an optional module, not the backbone.

### Gaps
- I found no benchmark that directly evaluates "related-topic recommendation" quality for agents (e.g., whether suggested related pages lead to better answers or discovery). The evidence is indirect, through multi-hop QA.
- No independent replications were found for LeanRAG, ArchRAG, KET-RAG, NodeRAG or SiReRAG.

## 5. Domain-specific knowledge bases: medicine (UMLS), law, finance, engineering/manufacturing manuals, science (Semantic Scholar, ORKG), enterprise

### Takeaway
Where a curated vocabulary exists (UMLS in medicine, statutory structure in law, standards in engineering), the winning pattern is to anchor the auto-built KB to it: link concepts to CUIs or statutory sections, use its synonyms for expansion and its hierarchy for navigation. Where none exists (most enterprises, e-commerce in emerging markets), LLMs bootstrap the taxonomy and glossary with human validation (OntoEKG, BEATS). Science has the most mature public infrastructure: S2's graph of 200M+ papers, ORKG ASK, Ai2 Scholar QA and PaperQA2 wiki generation.

### Cited Findings
- **Medicine / UMLS.** The UMLS Metathesaurus 2026AB release has 3,550,208 concepts (CUIs), 15.1M distinct strings, 196 contributing sources and 31 languages (V/official). — [NLM UMLS statistics](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/umls/knowledge_sources/metathesaurus/release/statistics.html). A UMLS-derived KG (3.4M concepts, 34.2M relations, in Neo4j) was used both for continual pretraining (BERT_UMLS) and for GraphRAG on PubMedQA/BioASQ (LREC 2026, AR). — [arXiv 2604.16422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16422). MedGraphRAG (Aug 2024) builds a "triple-linked" graph connecting user documents to credible medical sources and controlled vocabularies, with top-down retrieval plus bottom-up refinement. It reports results on 9 medical QA and 2 fact-check benchmarks (AR). — [arXiv 2408.04187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04187). UMLS-guided QE (BMQExpander) and CUICurate concept sets are in Section 2.
- **Law.** SAT-Graph models legal norms as structure-aware temporal KGs. Its API confines LLM uncertainty to intent translation, anchoring and synthesis, and makes hierarchical, temporal and causal traversal deterministic (Oct 2025, spec paper). — [arXiv 2510.06002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06002). A fair-use RAG prototype models precedents at statutory-factor level with citation-weighted graphs; evaluation is preliminary (2025). — [arXiv 2505.02164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02164). LegalGraphRAG (May 2026) applies multi-agent graph RAG to legal reasoning. — [arXiv 2605.28120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28120). Statutory definition extraction with scope is in Section 2.
- **Engineering / manufacturing manuals.** GEMS-RAG (Oct 2026) parses hierarchical standards (MUTCD), decomposes figures into visual entities linked to provisions, concepts and rules, and combines dense, sparse and visual retrieval with graph expansion (AR). — [arXiv 2610.33277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33277). Fincantieri's study (ICSE-SEIP 2026) on naval troubleshooting manuals found RAG helps operators find procedures but recommends cross-validating recommendations before acting (industry study). — [arXiv 2601.08706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08706). LARAG exploits hyperlinks in technical docs (Section 4).
- **Finance.** PolyRAG introduced a finance-domain retrieval benchmark and an ontology → KG → chunk pyramid (AR). — [arXiv 2407.21276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21276). Corpus2Skill tied on FinQA and lost on TatQA (homogeneous tabular), which indicates topic navigation does not suit numeric/tabular financial corpora (AR). — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572)
- **Science.** The Semantic Scholar Academic Graph has 200M+ papers, 80M+ authors and 2.4B+ citation edges, plus parsed text, TLDR summaries, embeddings and public APIs (2023). — [arXiv 2301.10140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10140). ORKG ASK (TIB; Dec 2024) combines semantic search, LLM extraction and the ORKG; it is production-ready and permissively licensed. — [arXiv 2412.04977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04977). LLMs for ORKG structured summaries ("research contribution" property extraction) were evaluated against expert-curated properties (2024). — [arXiv 2405.02105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02105). PaperQA2/WikiCrow and Ai2 Scholar QA are in Section 3.
- **Enterprise.** Glean describes its graph as content (docs, messages, tickets), people (identities, roles, teams) and activity (edits, comments, searches, clicks), with a per-employee personal graph announced Sep 2025. Its agents use it for permission-aware context. There is no independent technical detail (V). — [Glean blog](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-ai-knowledge-graph); [BusinessWire Sep 25 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250925784461/en/Glean-Introduces-Third-Generation-AI-Assistant-New-Enterprise-Graph-to-Enable-the-Superintelligent-Enterprise). OntoEKG, business-glossary matching, MetaGen and DocuSearch are in Sections 1–2. Corpus2Skill's best case was an enterprise customer-support KB (WixQA).

### Inferences
- The toolkit should support pluggable **anchor vocabularies** (UMLS/MeSH, legal code structure, product catalogs, ISO/industry standards, FIBO-style ontologies) loaded as SKOS. The induced taxonomy and glossary should map to them with `exactMatch`/`closeMatch`, so domain users get familiar navigation and query expansion gains synonyms for free.
- Legal and regulatory KBs need temporal versioning and scope on concepts and definitions (SAT-Graph, U.S. Code scope). A wiki page for a rule must say which version and jurisdiction it describes.
- Activity signals (Glean) are a strong enterprise "related topics" signal (co-viewed or co-edited pages) that pure-text methods miss. The toolkit could ingest them as edges where available.

### Gaps
- I did not verify FIBO (finance ontology) or any 2025–26 finance KB paper in depth. Finance-specific knowledge-organization evidence here is thin.
- No independent evaluation of Glean's graph or of other enterprise KG products was found. Enterprise evidence is mostly vendor or single-deployment.

## 6. Most effective layering of raw chunks + synthesized pages + taxonomy/graph for agent search, and cost

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a **four-layer, evidence-anchored design**: (L0) raw chunks with hybrid search, always the ground truth and the fallback; (L1) a concept/glossary layer (SKOS concepts, aliases, definitions) driving expansion and linking; (L2) a navigation layer (multi-facet taxonomy with INDEX pages, related-topic links); (L3) a synthesized layer of entity, topic and FAQ pages built from claim+evidence pairs with source pointers. Add L2/L3 selectively. Navigation wins on bounded, topical corpora (Corpus2Skill 5/11 wins) and loses on open-domain, tabular or long-extractive ones. Under strong agentic search, synthesized layers mainly cut tokens (38%) rather than raise accuracy. Compile costs are modest per document ($3.33/6k docs, Clio about $0.0005 per item), but query-time costs can be 13–22× single-shot retrieval when navigation context repeats.

### Cited Findings
- **Navigation vs flat retrieval: scope.** "Corpus navigation is not a universal replacement for retrieval ... It helps on single-domain corpora with a recoverable topical taxonomy, but flat retrieval remains preferable on open-domain factoid pools or homogeneous-tabular corpora" (AR). — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572)
- **Query-time cost.** Corpus2Skill costs $0.153/query (1.9× agentic RAG, 13–22× single-shot) and $0.302 without prompt caching. Navigation context repeats in every call (AR). — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572). LLM-Wiki reads 2.5–3.9 pages per query at about dense-RAG latency (AR). — [arXiv 2605.25480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480)
- **Compile cost.** Corpus2Skill: $3.33 (6k docs) and $54.1 (50k docs), about 19.5 min on an RTX 4090 for embeddings (AR). Clio: $48.81 per 100k conversations for facet extraction, clustering and labeling (AR). TopicGPT: about $88–155 per corpus with GPT-4/3.5 in 2023 prices (AR). LazyGraphRAG: indexing equal to vector RAG and 0.1% of GraphRAG (V). — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572); [arXiv 2412.13678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678); [arXiv 2311.01449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01449); [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)
- **Break-even logic.** A compiler costing ten query-equivalents pays back after about 26 queries at a 43% evidence-sufficiency rate. Break-even sufficiency is about 2% (V, analytic plus measured). — [arXiv 2610.41172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172)
- **Layered retrieval designs.** PolyRAG's waterfall (ontology → KG → chunks, stop when confident) (AR). — [arXiv 2407.21276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21276). MedGraphRAG's U-Retrieval (top-down precise retrieval, bottom-up refinement) (AR). — [arXiv 2408.04187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04187). SiReRAG flattens similarity and relatedness trees into one pool (AR). — [arXiv 2412.06206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06206). GraphRAG-Bench and other independent results on when graph layers help (multi-hop, summarization) vs hurt (simple facts) are in the earlier notes file.
- **Agent-native interface.** Corpus2Skill serves SKILL.md/INDEX.md with progressive disclosure through ls/cat/grep (AR). LLM-Wiki exposes `wiki_search`/`wiki_read` (AR). The Karpathy gist uses index.md plus optional qmd hybrid search over an MCP server (V). — [arXiv 2604.14572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572); [arXiv 2605.25480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480); [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- **Structure without LLM summaries.** NavTree's zero-LLM tree navigation matched or beat abstractive RAPTOR at matched cost. — [arXiv 2610.06902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06902)
- **Failure modes to design for:** routing errors at the top level (Corpus2Skill); recompilation on churn (Corpus2Skill, LLM-Wiki); dangling links as the dominant compile error (LLM-Wiki); summary drift, frozen errors and entrenchment (gist critiques; Memory-as-Metabolism); hallucinated generated questions (Doc2Query--); source bias transfer and over-association in generated articles (STORM); LLM-judge bias inflating claimed gains (unbiased GraphRAG eval). — sources as cited in Sections 1–4.

### Inferences (recommended design for the toolkit)
1. **L0 Evidence store (always on).** Chunks/propositions with hybrid BM25+dense search and stable doc/span IDs (chunking itself is another track). Every higher-layer object must point back to L0 spans.
2. **L1 Concept & glossary layer (cheap, high value).**
   - Extract candidate terms, acronyms and definitions with spans, and cluster aliases.
   - Map to anchor vocabularies where they exist (UMLS, statutes, catalogs) and store as SKOS.
   - Use it for query expansion and boosting (BMQExpander/MetaGen pattern), entity linking, and a `define()` tool.
   - Verify definitions against spans (NLI) before promotion (SciDef).
3. **L2 Navigation layer (conditional).**
   - Build multi-facet trees via embed → recursive k-means → LLM naming and inclusion criteria → verify/repartition (Clio/Corpus2Skill/TaxoAdapt). Keep branching moderate (well below 20).
   - Use soft multi-assignment for borderline docs.
   - Emit INDEX.md pages per node and SKOS broader/narrower.
   - Turn it on only if cluster coherence passes a ProxAnn-style check and the corpus is topical, not tabular or open-domain (Corpus2Skill scope rule).
   - Related-topic links: co-occurrence concept graph + taxonomy siblings + author links + embedding kNN, ranked with PPR (HippoRAG/LazyGraphRAG/LARAG).
4. **L3 Synthesized pages (selective, evidence-first).**
   - Write entity, concept and topic pages and FAQ entries as claim + verbatim evidence (RAGFlow, ASKS).
   - Maintain them with ingest, lint and log operations (Karpathy/LLM-Wiki Error Book).
   - Generate pages only for high-traffic or high-centrality concepts, and use query logs to prioritize (WikiLoop's downstream-utility idea).
   - Keep generated Q&A in a separate, filtered index (Doc2Query--, QuOTE).
   - Run faithfulness gates (citation recall/precision, FActScore-style atomic checks) before publishing.
5. **Agent interface.** Expose `search(query, facets)`, `read(page|doc)`, `browse(node)`, `related(concept)`, `define(term)` via MCP, with progressive disclosure, and always allow drop-through to L0 search (flat retrieval wins on many corpora).
6. **Optional modules.** GraphRAG-style community reports for corpus-level theme questions, and LazyGraphRAG-style concept graphs where budget is tight. Neither should be the backbone, given cost, maintenance status and evaluation-bias evidence.
7. **Cost expectations.** L1 and L2 cost cents to tens of dollars per 10k docs on current LLM prices (extrapolated from Clio and Corpus2Skill). L3 scales with the pages generated, and no reliable public number was found. The bigger cost risk is query-time context repetition, so use prompt caching and claim-level leaves.

### Gaps
- No independent head-to-head of the full layered design (glossary + taxonomy + wiki + chunks) against flat hybrid retrieval on a heterogeneous enterprise corpus was found. The recommendation is assembled from component studies, mostly author-reported.
- Incremental maintenance cost (re-clustering, page regeneration on document churn) is unmeasured. Corpus2Skill assumes static corpora, and LLM-Wiki leaves dynamic corpora to future work. ASKS shows stable hub structure under additive ingest on only 56 papers.
- The cost extrapolations in item 7 are inferences from two papers' figures, not measurements.
