# Compile Knowledge Into Units Agents Can Search

The most effective way to turn unstructured, heterogeneous content into a knowledge base that AI agents search is to **compile it into typed, versioned knowledge units** and index them with mostly cheap, deterministic methods. The unit types are concept and glossary entries, topic and entity pages, rule cards, procedures, and articles or FAQs. Each unit points back to verbatim source spans and carries access-control, validity and authority metadata. Agents reach the units through a markdown file tree with a small always-loaded index and a handful of tools (search, read, grep, related, define, plus procedure and rule lookups), not through dozens of connector-specific tools. This store differs from a deterministic typed database: it returns ranked evidence that the agent reads and judges, with citations, rather than exact answers to predicates. The two must coexist. Counts, filters, joins and changing attributes belong in typed tables. Any rule that gates an action needs an executable binding evaluated outside the LLM. Everything that needs interpretation, explanation or navigation belongs in the searchable knowledge base. The evidence favors simple, structural methods over elaborate LLM pipelines at almost every layer. In a preregistered study, a free heading-path prefix matched LLM enrichment once a strong embedder or reranker was in place ([Mangla 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)). Structure-aligned chunking explained **73–88% of the retrieval gain** on legal corpora ([StrucChunk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26313)). Curated skills raised agent pass rates by **16.6 points**, while skills the agents wrote for themselves fell *below* the no-skill baseline ([SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670)). Most failures in deployment come from the knowledge itself rather than the retriever. One outdated passage in context cut Llama-3.1-70B from **89 to 73** ([HoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04800)), and outdated evidence flipped **17–91%** of otherwise-correct answers ([Stale-Document Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31342)). Salesforce traced many of its own agents' apparent hallucinations to conflicting, unlinked documents ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/first-year-agentforce-customer-zero/)). Retrieval also cannot deliver policy compliance on its own. Frontier agents reached only about **40% pass^1 even when handed the right policy documents** ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-knowledge)), while compiled pre-call guards raised pass^10 from 0.227 to **0.500**, against 0.273 for putting the policy in the prompt ([ToolGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16459)). Commercial platforms have converged on this pattern: connectors with ACL sync, hybrid indexes, procedures as versioned and tested artifacts, and mining of failed conversations into knowledge-base edits under human approval. Their outcome numbers are almost all self-reported. No independent study yet evaluates the full layered design end to end, so every team needs its own evaluation set before it optimizes anything.

*Evidence base.* This report synthesizes eight research tracks: indexing and enrichment; procedures and SOPs; rules and policies; domain knowledge organization; agentic search and memory; quality and lifecycle; enterprise platforms; and open-source stacks. Their manifests contain 907 entries, which reduce to **856 distinct sources** (578 papers and 278 web sources such as documentation, pricing pages and engineering blogs) after de-duplicating by arXiv ID, DOI and URL. A combined catalog of sources is kept in `papers/README.md` and `papers/manifest.csv`. PDFs are stored locally only and can be rebuilt by passing the research-notes folders to `scripts/download_papers.py`. Throughout, **independent** means the result was measured by a party that did not build the winning system. **Author-reported** means the number comes from the method's own paper. **Vendor-reported** marks product documentation, vendor-run studies and vendor-hosted customer stories, none of which have been audited. Most 2026 papers cited here are arXiv preprints without peer review. Status is as of November 2026.

## A searchable knowledge base answers "what applies here", a typed database answers "how many"

The companion report on deterministic retrieval argued that an LLM should run once, at ingestion, to fill a closed, versioned schema that agents then query with exact predicates. That design wins whenever the question can be written as a predicate. Extraction followed by SQL beat chunk RAG on intersection queries (**50.9 vs 6.0 recall**) and on counting (**51.2 vs 30.0**) ([QO-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04646)). The questions in this report are different. Agents need to find related topics, learn what a domain term means, work out which policy clause governs a situation, and follow a procedure. These are open-ended needs. The schema cannot anticipate them, the answer is a passage to read rather than a value to return, and the agent applies judgement. A searchable knowledge base is built for this. It ranks instead of filtering, tolerates paraphrase, returns citable evidence and degrades gracefully when the question is vague. It also has the opposite weaknesses. Microsoft states that Copilot's semantic index does not help queries with no topic ("find bugs assigned to"), multi-parameter queries, or count queries ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/extensibility/overview-copilot-connector)). On derived state computed by rules over changing events, **every memory system tested fell behind plain BM25**. The same reader reached 96% when given the supporting events, which led the authors to recommend storing rules with versions and maintaining derived state through recorded dependencies ([MemDerive](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25373)).

The two stores should therefore share one spine and divide the work by question type. The spine has four parts: stable identifiers, source spans, access-control lists, and validity and version fields. The division follows from the evidence in both reports. Several knowledge types straddle the boundary, and they show how the stores interlock. A policy is a rule card in the knowledge base, because agents must find and explain it. Its numeric thresholds and eligibility logic are also an executable binding, because LLMs miscompute even when given the correct rule ([RuleArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08972)). A table inside a manual is indexed as a captioned record so it can be found, and its rows land in typed tables so they can be filtered ([TableRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04739)). A procedure is a searchable unit and also a state machine whose current step a runtime tracks.

| Content or question | Searchable knowledge base | Typed database or executable layer |
|---|---|---|
| Concepts, definitions, explanations, "how does X work" | SKOS concept entries and topic pages, hybrid search, `define` | Concept IDs reused as join keys |
| What a policy says, how to interpret it, which clause applies | Rule cards with citation closure and hard validity filters | Thresholds, eligibility and authorization as DMN, ASP, Cedar or Rego bindings |
| How to do a task (SOP, runbook, playbook) | Procedure unit with "when to use" description, rendered as a skill | Step state, preconditions and tool constraints in a runtime |
| Tables inside documents | Table record (caption, headers, description) for finding | Rows as typed tables for filtering and aggregation |
| Counts, "assigned to X", multi-attribute filters, joins | Not suitable | SQL, semantic layer or parameterized tools |
| Volatile facts (prices, statuses, balances) | Not indexed; served live | Typed tables, APIs, derived state with lineage |
| Past resolutions, tickets, FAQs | Articles and FAQ units with generated questions | Case metadata (dates, products, owners) |
| Agent-learned lessons | Proposed pages awaiting review | Usage and outcome statistics |

The routing rule for agents follows directly. A question with a computable answer goes to a data tool, which returns a value and a snapshot ID. A question that needs evidence goes to the knowledge base, which returns cited passages. A question that ends in a side-effecting action goes through an enforcement gate that reads the same rule records as the knowledge base. The earlier report's "labelled fallback" from exact queries to unstructured retrieval is the same seam, seen from the other side.

## Structure-led chunks, a free prefix and tuned hybrid retrieval carry most of the indexing gain

Independent studies from 2024 to 2026 point in one direction: cheap, structural indexing gets most of the achievable quality, and each LLM step must justify itself on the knowledge base's own queries. The rest of this section treats segmentation, enrichment, granularity, representation and semi-structured content in turn.

### Simple segmentation along the document's own structure wins for corpus-wide search

Large multi-method comparisons now agree. A significance-tested study of 8 strategies on corpora of up to 1M documents found that computationally expensive methods "rarely provide consistent gains". It recommended token chunking as the default and a title prefix as the cheapest improvement (independent; [Caspari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16586)). A benchmark of 36 segmentation methods across 6 domains and 5 embedders ranked paragraph-group chunking first and fixed-size character chunking last ([Shaukat et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06976)). An industrial study found that chunk overlap gave "no measurable benefit" while adding indexing cost ([Bennani & Moslonka](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14123)). The Vectara–UW study concluded that semantic chunking's compute cost is "not justified". Semantic chunkers won mainly on artificially stitched multi-topic documents, and embedding quality mattered more than the chunker ([Qu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13070)). In Chroma's token-level evaluation, the best and worst chunkers were only 9 recall points apart, and the popular embedding-breakpoint chunker came last (vendor-run; [Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/evaluating-chunking)).

The domain studies closest to policies and SOPs favor structure even more strongly. On the German Civil Code, section and subsection chunks gave the highest recall. Methods that "override this structure", including contextual chunking, RAPTOR and LLM segmentation, did worse and cost more ([Prior et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19806)). On oil-and-gas manuals, structure-aware chunking beat fixed, recursive and semantic chunking at lower cost ([Taiwo & Yusoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24556)). On Portuguese administrative documents, Docling with hierarchical splitting reached **94.1%** accuracy, above both hand-curated Markdown (91.3%) and a naive PDF loader (86.2%). Table questions showed a **33-point** gap between basic and hierarchical splitting, and "metadata enrichment and hierarchy-aware chunking contributed more to accuracy than the conversion framework alone" ([dos Santos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04948)). NVIDIA found page-level chunks had the best and most stable average for visually dense PDFs, although per-dataset winners varied (0.648 average; vendor-run; [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/finding-the-best-chunking-strategy-for-accurate-ai-responses/)).

The fashionable alternatives work only in narrow regimes. Late chunking was roughly neutral for some embedders and collapsed for others: BGE-M3 fell to 0.070 NDCG@5 against 0.246 for early chunking ([Merola & Singh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19754)). It lowered Qasper recall by 11.4 points against plain header-led chunks ([Mangla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)) and had near-worst Recall@100 at scale ([Caspari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16586)). LumberChunker-style LLM segmentation is best for *in-document* needle search over long narratives without headings. It loses on in-corpus search, and it processes about 1.11 documents per second against roughly 1,854 for paragraph splitting (independent reproduction; [Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16974)). Boundaries also have an operational cost. After real Wikipedia edits, fixed-size chunking re-embedded **57–68%** of a document against 38–56% for recursive chunking, with evidence recall within 2 points ([Bian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31019)). For living policy documents, content-aware boundaries are therefore cheaper to maintain as well as better to retrieve.

The default that follows is to split at headings first, keep tables, lists, procedure steps and FAQ pairs atomic, merge small siblings under the same heading up to about 256–512 tokens, split oversized sections at sentence boundaries, and drop overlap. Page-level units suit slide decks and visually dense PDFs. Chunking should be a cheap, re-runnable view over a persisted document tree, never a one-way decision baked into the index.

### Enrichment restores document identity, mostly for lexical retrieval

Enrichment helps chiefly by putting a document's identity and missing vocabulary back into each chunk. That matters most for BM25 and for collections of near-identical documents such as contracts, filings and policy versions. Anthropic's contextual retrieval had Claude write 50–100 tokens of context per chunk. It cut the top-20 retrieval failure rate from 5.7% to 3.7% with embeddings, 2.9% with BM25 added, and 1.9% with reranking, at about **$1.02 per million document tokens** with prompt caching (vendor-run; [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). The most rigorous independent test is a preregistered study that withdrew its own earlier results. A single 7B-model call producing titles, summaries, keywords and questions beat a free section-path prefix by 5.5 recall points under hybrid retrieval. It was statistically equivalent to contextual retrieval, which consumed **5.8–8.7× the input tokens**. Under a strong embedder or a reranker, the enrichment gain fell within equivalence margins, and the free prefix with a strong retriever beat hybrid-plus-enrichment by 3.4–6.3 points ([Mangla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)). On a 1,600-query production Markdown knowledge base, a zero-LLM title-chain prefix raised MRR@5 by **23.8%** ([Yang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00824)). Prepending one 150-character document summary per document cut legal document-level retrieval mismatch on ContractNLI from above 95% to about 19% ([Reuter et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06999)).

Generated questions help with question-style queries but need control. QuOTE stored each generated question as its own vector pointing to the parent chunk. It gained 6.8–17.2 points on SQuAD, with diminishing returns beyond 10–15 questions per chunk and regressions for some embedders (author-reported; [QuOTE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10976)). Generated queries also hallucinate. Filtering them with a relevance model gave up to +16% effectiveness, −23% query time and −33% index size ([Doc2Query--](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266)). Doc2Query++ adds that concatenating questions into chunk text adds noise for dense retrieval, so question signals belong in a separate index ([Kuo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09557)). A further distinction matters. Text that helps the retriever *find* a chunk is not text that helps the reader *use* it. Across 24,000 responses, attaching metadata, structure or confidence scores to passages given to the model mostly *reduced* accuracy ([Zerhoudi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29645)). Index-side enrichment and reader-side context should therefore be stored and governed separately.

The cost-effective order is: a deterministic breadcrumb prefix ("Document › Section › Subsection" plus type and effective date) always; one summary per document, prepended to every chunk's embedding input; one call per chunk producing keywords, entities, acronyms and filtered questions, stored as separate fields and vectors; and full contextual retrieval only if it beats these steps with the retriever actually deployed. Every step should be re-benchmarked after an embedder or reranker upgrade, because the gains shrink as the retriever improves.

### Match small, read large, and treat summary trees as navigation

The robust granularity pattern is to decouple the retrieval unit from the reading unit. The system matches on sentences, questions or summaries and returns the enclosing section or page. In an independent comparison, sentence-window retrieval gave the best retrieval precision, while multi-query and MMR did not help ([ARAGOG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01037)). Proposition indexing raised Recall@5 by 12.0 points for an unsupervised retriever but only 2.6 for DPR, at **six times the vectors** and about 500 GPU-hours to decompose Wikipedia (author-reported; [Dense X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)). A 2026 reproduction found propositions the weakest in-corpus unit ([Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16974)).

LLM summary trees deserve particular caution because they are popular. RAPTOR reported large gains on long-document QA, and 4% of its sampled summary nodes contained hallucinations (author-reported; [RAPTOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059)). Independent tables are less kind. With a Llama-3.3-70B reader, RAPTOR averaged **48.8 F1 against 57.0** for plain NV-Embed-v2 dense retrieval ([HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)). A deterministic segment tree built with **zero LLM calls** and used purely for navigation tied the strongest flat baseline and beat abstractive RAPTOR at every multi-chunk budget. Its authors concluded the benefit "can come from navigation rather than the generated summary content" ([NavTree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06902)). For a knowledge base with headings, the document's own heading tree is a free RAPTOR. Persisting it as data (node ID, parent, path, page range) serves three purposes at once: breadcrumb prefixes, small-to-big expansion, and a table of contents that agents can browse.

### Keep a lexical channel, choose a strong embedder, validate the reranker

Three representation choices matter most. The first is a strong embedder, which outweighs chunking in several studies ([Qu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13070)). The second is a lexical channel that is never dropped. Google DeepMind proved that single-vector embedders are bounded in which top-k sets they can represent. On its LIMIT task, every state-of-the-art embedder scored below 20 Recall@100, while BM25 scored 93.6 ([LIMIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21038)). In Microsoft's customer data, vector search scored 11.7 NDCG@3 on keyword-style queries against 79.2 for keyword search, and hybrid plus semantic reranking lifted overall NDCG@3 from 43.8 (vector only) to 60.1 (vendor-run; [Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/azure-ai-foundry-blog/azure-ai-search-outperforming-vector-search-with-hybrid/ba-p/3929167)). Codes, acronyms, CVE numbers, policy IDs and product versions are everywhere in SOP and policy knowledge bases, so field-boosted lexical search is mandatory. The third is a reranker, which must be validated on the domain: an MS MARCO-trained cross-encoder *reduced* precision on a scientific corpus ([SciRet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03860)).

Fusion must be tuned rather than assumed. In Vespa's own NFCorpus tutorial, BM25 alone (0.3210 nDCG@10) beat a small dense model (0.3077), and normalized linear fusion (0.3423) beat RRF (0.3233) ([Vespa](https://docs.vespa.ai/en/learn/tutorials/hybrid-search.html)). The preregistered enrichment study traced part of enrichment's apparent value to repairing poorly tuned fusion ([Mangla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533)). Domain fine-tuning with synthetic queries roughly doubled FinanceBench Recall@10 for a small embedder (0.293 to 0.552) but gave no gain on a documentation corpus where recall was already high. Databricks also reported that MTEB scores did not predict enterprise performance (vendor-run; [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/improving-retrieval-and-rag-embedding-model-finetuning)). A newer class of contextualized chunk embedders builds document context into the embedding itself, which could make LLM enrichment partly redundant. InSeNT scored 75.6 on ConTEB against 52.0 for its base model ([Conti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24782)). Voyage claims +14.24% over OpenAI-v3-large at $0.18 per million tokens, but its page reports figures inconsistently (vendor-reported; [Voyage](https://blog.voyageai.com/2025/07/23/voyage-context-3/)). These embedders are worth an A/B test, not a default.

### Semi-structured content needs unit-specific indexing

FAQs should index the question separately from the answer, because query-to-question similarity and query-to-answer relevance are complementary signals ([Sakata et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02851)). Tables need a record per table (title, caption, headers, generated description), row-group chunks with repeated headers, and a link to the typed table. Dense retrievers outperform BM25 on tables but suffer badly when titles and metadata are missing ([TARGET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11545)), and large tables are better served by schema and cell retrieval than by chunking ([TableRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04739)). For slides, page-image retrieval (ColPali) scored 82.7 nDCG@10 against 54.3 for BM25 over OCR. VLM captions with hybrid search and a reranker reached 83.9 using about a third of ColPali's storage (4.21 GB vs 12.9 GB), though large rerankers added 7–14 seconds per query ([arXiv 2509.15211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15211)). For wikis and HTML, the decisive fix is to complete each evidence unit with its page title, section path and table headers, because naive chunking separates tables and infoboxes from the headers that give them meaning ([Tessera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37080)). Scanned diagrams such as P&IDs and flowcharts defeated every text method tested ([Taiwo & Yusoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24556)) and need a dedicated diagram-to-graph path.

No study measures this full stack end to end on an SOP or policy corpus with agent-style queries. The recommended design is assembled from component evidence, and the honest way to use it is as a set of defaults that an evaluation gate on the knowledge base's own queries can switch on or off.

## Concepts, rules and procedures each need their own unit, index and access path

Treating all knowledge as interchangeable chunks is the main design mistake the evidence warns against. Domain concepts, rules and procedures fail in different ways and are used in different ways. Concepts are browsed and expanded. Rules must be found completely, at the right version, and sometimes enforced. Procedures are followed step by step. Each type therefore gets its own canonical unit, its own indexing emphasis and its own access pattern.

### Domain knowledge: an alias table first, a navigation layer when the corpus is topical, evidence-first pages last

The highest-value domain artifact is also the cheapest: a **glossary and alias table** stored as SKOS concepts. Each concept has a preferred label; alternate labels for synonyms, acronyms and legacy product names; hidden labels for misspellings; a definition with its source span; a scope note; and broader, narrower and related links ([W3C SKOS](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/)). The same table drives three things: query expansion and boosting, entity linking for pages and graphs, and a `define(term)` tool. Thesaurus-guided query expansion over UMLS gained up to **22.1% NDCG@10** over sparse baselines and hallucinated less than LLM-only expansion (author-reported; [BMQExpander](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11784)). A production telecom search system reports that dense retrieval "frequently performs poorly" on queries mixing jargon and vendor acronyms, and it fuses vector, BM25 and knowledge-graph-neighbour results to compensate (single deployment; [DocuSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01617)). Automatic definition extraction is not reliable enough to publish unreviewed. The best of 16 LLMs scored only **0.397** at set level, and the highest-coverage configuration matched 86.4% of gold definitions only by over-generating ([SciDef](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05413)). Extracted definitions should therefore enter as candidates with spans and be promoted only after an entailment check, with expert sign-off in regulated domains. Legal definitions also need a jurisdictional scope field, as in "for purposes of this chapter" ([arXiv 2504.16353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16353)).

A **taxonomy or navigation layer** helps agents only when the corpus has a recoverable topical structure. The workable recipe is to embed, cluster, have an LLM name and describe each cluster with an inclusion criterion, recurse, then verify. Anthropic's Clio recovered known categories with **94% accuracy** at about $49 per 100,000 items (author-reported; [Clio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678)). Microsoft's TnT-LLM adds the cost lesson: use the LLM to design the taxonomy and label a sample, then train cheap classifiers to tag everything else ([TnT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12173)). Letting an LLM invent the hierarchy from its own knowledge does not work on specialist domains. Across 18 LLMs and 10 taxonomies, models failed to capture specialized taxonomies and leaf-level entities (independent benchmark; [TaxoGlimpse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11131)). The best agent-side evidence is Corpus2Skill. It compiles a corpus into a forest of SKILL.md and INDEX.md files that the agent navigates with ls, cat and grep. On a 6,221-article customer-support knowledge base it reached 0.456 token F1 against 0.364 for dense retrieval. Across 11 datasets, however, it won on only 5, tied on 3 and lost on 3, losing on open-domain, tabular and long-extractive corpora. It also cost **$0.153 per query against $0.007** for dense retrieval, and wide trees (branching factor 20) degraded sharply (author-reported; [Corpus2Skill](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14572)). Navigation should therefore be gated by a cluster-coherence check, which LLM proxies can now run at human-equivalent quality ([ProxAnn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00828)). Separate facet trees (product, process, document type, regulation, org unit) suit heterogeneous enterprise content better than a single hierarchy ([TaxoAdapt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10737)).

**Synthesized pages**, the "LLM wiki" or knowledge-compilation pattern, became the field's main idea in 2026. Karpathy's gist describes three layers: immutable raw sources, an LLM-maintained interlinked markdown wiki, and a schema file that makes the model a "disciplined wiki maintainer". It has three operations: ingest (one source touching 10–15 pages), query (with good answers filed back as pages) and lint. He claims an index file alone "works surprisingly well at moderate scale (~100 sources, ~hundreds of pages)" (opinion; [Karpathy](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)). The academic LLM-Wiki reported multi-hop F1 of 0.839 on HotpotQA against 0.805 for HippoRAG 2 and 0.764 for dense retrieval. Removing progressive traversal cost 11.7–13.8 points, and dangling links made up 55.8–63.8% of compile errors (author-reported; [LLM-Wiki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480)). The strongest counter-evidence is vendor-run but careful. Under a competent BM25-and-grep agent harness, an uncompiled baseline reached 91.0% on FRAMES, and RAPTOR summaries, wikis and graphs were **accuracy-neutral**. Their measurable value was a 38% average cut in query tokens, and only when leaves were claim-plus-verbatim-evidence pairs rather than prose ([RAGFlow/ECNU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172)). Faithfulness is the main risk of generated prose: about 15% of STORM's sentences were unsupported, mostly through "improper inferential linking" ([STORM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207)). The defensible design is **evidence-first pages**. Each page holds atomic claims with verbatim source spans, narrative is a rendering of those claims, and pages are generated only for high-traffic or high-centrality concepts. Such pages are a navigation and context-compression layer, not an accuracy guarantee.

**Graphs** earn their place for "find related topics", not as an exact-query store. Associative retrieval by Personalized PageRank over a concept graph (HippoRAG) and explicit links between community summaries, so they are not "semantic islands" ([LeanRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10391)), are the patterns with evidence. Many graph-RAG gains shrink under fair evaluation. An unbiased re-evaluation cut LightRAG's reported 66.70% win rate over naive RAG to 39.06%, with naive RAG slightly ahead (independent; [arXiv 2506.06331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06331)). The companion data-lake report found query-time token cost ranging from about 1.15× vector RAG for HippoRAG 2 to about 377× for GraphRAG global search ([GraphRAG-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)), and Microsoft's GraphRAG repository now describes itself as largely in maintenance mode ([microsoft/graphrag](https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag)). A cheap related-topics feature can be assembled from glossary-term co-occurrence (LazyGraphRAG indexes at 0.1% of GraphRAG's cost, vendor-reported; [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)), taxonomy siblings, author hyperlinks and cross-references, and embedding neighbours, ranked by PageRank from the current page. Dropbox reached the same conclusion in production: it kept its people-and-content graph out of a graph database and materialized it as text "knowledge bundles" in the ordinary index ([Dropbox](https://dropbox.tech/machine-learning/vp-josh-clemm-knowledge-graphs-mcp-and-dspy-dash)). Where a curated vocabulary exists (UMLS with 3.55M concepts, statutory structure, product catalogs, industry standards), the induced glossary and taxonomy should map onto it with `exactMatch` and `closeMatch` rather than compete with it ([NLM](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/umls/knowledge_sources/metathesaurus/release/statistics.html)).

### Rules and policies: rule cards make them searchable, selective bindings make them enforceable

The canonical rule unit is an **NL rule card**. It holds the atomic rule text, modality (obligation, permission, prohibition, definition, exception), subject or role, conditions, thresholds, exceptions, defined terms used, resolved cross-references, jurisdiction, effective dates, source span and extraction confidence. This tier can be reliably automated when the pipeline segments along the document's structure, decomposes into atomic rules, judges and repairs. De Jure scores each extracted unit on 19 dimensions with an LLM judge and repairs low scorers, with quality peaking within three iterations (author-reported; [De Jure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02276)). RegReAct found that single-pass LLMs "hallucinate structural elements, lose hierarchical relationships, and fail to resolve inter-document dependencies". Its seven-agent pipeline extracted 242 activities and more than 4,800 criteria from EU Taxonomy acts ([RegReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12054)). In a four-month parallel run at a financial institution, a multi-task obligation extractor processed 9,847 regulatory updates at an estimated 96.0% recall and 90.7% precision (deployment numbers reported by the system's authors; [ComplianceNLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23585)).

Translating rules into **executable code** is far less reliable, and headline numbers depend heavily on the metric chosen. One NL-to-Rego pipeline reported a 95.3% compile rate ([Prose2Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15799)). Under a strict criterion (compiles, lints, and passes positive and negative tests), the same authors measured **50.3%** correctness with structured decomposition and 15.3% with a single prompt ([Gupta & Sreenivasamurthy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24036)). Against 95 production decision models from the Dutch permit platform, generated models matched gold outcomes on only **51–53%** of scenarios, and "structural similarity does not guarantee outcome equivalence" ([Graus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17153)). AutoCedar warns that "a generated policy can compile and read correctly while granting access that no one approved". It converged on all 221 benchmark tasks only by first having humans review "intent atoms" that the verifier then checks against ([AutoCedar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03656)). An independent use of AWS's policy formalizer on τ²-bench airline rules found under-constrained rules, such as permitting cancellation within 24 hours without forbidding it outside that window ([Winston et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20449)). Executable bindings should therefore be selective, generated through review, schema validation, compilation, positive and negative tests and solver analysis, and signed off by a human.

Where bindings exist, the **neuro-symbolic split** wins: the LLM extracts facts and a symbolic engine decides. IBM's policy-as-logic translated each policy once into an answer-set program. Per query, an LLM extracted JSON facts and clingo solved. On RuleArena airline rules this scored 0.61–1.00 against at most 0.38 for policy-in-prompt and 0.40 for LLM-written policy code. It was robust to perturbation and used about 10× fewer tokens, but gave no advantage on subjective HR moderation, and nearly all remaining errors were fact-extraction errors ([Nair et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11905)). Compiling radiology guidelines into a decision graph traversed deterministically raised accuracy from 57.3% to **90.8%**, above GPT-4o RAG at 80.1% ([GDGraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40327)). One gap matters for enforcement design. Frontier models gave the right verdict 89–92% of the time when facts sufficed, but only **22–40%** correctly abstained when facts were missing ([DeoGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38687)). Gates must therefore make "insufficient facts, escalate" an explicit third outcome.

Standards already divide the representation work, and a rule knowledge base should reuse them rather than invent a format.

| Representation | Status | Use in a rule knowledge base |
|---|---|---|
| Akoma Ntoso, ELI-style IDs | OASIS Standard 2018 | Provision hierarchy, Work/Expression versioning, point-in-time validity ([OASIS](https://docs.oasis-open.org/legaldocml/akn-core/v1.0/akn-core-v1.0-part1-vocabulary.html)) |
| LegalRuleML Core 1.0 | OASIS Standard 2021 | Deontic modality, defeasibility and override, temporal status, jurisdiction ([OASIS](https://docs.oasis-open.org/legalruleml/legalruleml-core-spec/v1.0/legalruleml-core-spec-v1.0.html)) |
| ODRL 2.2 | W3C Recommendation 2018 | Permission, prohibition and duty over assets ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/)) |
| DMN 1.6 | OMG, adopted Sep 2026 | Business decision tables editable by owners ([OMG](https://www.omg.org/spec/DMN)) |
| Cedar | AWS, open source, Lean-proved core | Fast, analysable permit/forbid on tool calls ([Cutler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04651)) |
| OPA/Rego | CNCF graduated | Datalog-like rules over JSON inputs ([OPA](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/)) |
| Catala | Inria, Apache-2.0 | Statute-aligned computation with default logic ([Merigoux et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03198)) |
| CQL / CPG-on-FHIR | HL7 | Computable clinical guidelines ([HL7](https://cql.hl7.org/)) |
| OSCAL | NIST | Machine-readable security control catalogs ([NIST](https://pages.nist.gov/OSCAL/)) |

Production authorization engines cover only the permit/forbid subset. Obligations, waivers and meta-policy conflict resolution need a deontic layer above them ([AgenticRei](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19464)).

**Retrieving the applicable rule set** is the main bottleneck, and three techniques carry the measured gains. The first is structure-aligned units with cross-references resolved before embedding. StrucChunk doubled LegalBench-RAG character recall@8 (**48.18% vs 23.59%**), with chunking accounting for 73–88% of the gain ([StrucChunk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26313)). The second is **citation closure**: expanding each hit to the provisions, definitions and exceptions it references. Existing systems saturate on flat rule sets such as HIPAA but fail on multi-tier authority structures without it ([RegOps-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29742)). The third is **temporal validity as a hard filter**. On German statutory questions about post-cutoff amendments, Claude Sonnet 3.7 scored 24.35% unaided, 63.48% with web search and **83.5–87.0%** with as-of-date extraction plus a version filter. Removing the filter caused a "substantial drop" ([Prior et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23497)). Authoritative point-in-time sources exist for some regimes; the US eCFR versioner API returns per-section versions with amendment dates ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/versions/title-12.json?part=1026&section=1026.18)). Evaluation needs gold rule IDs. A closed-book model scored within 0.011 of a full pipeline on the reference-free RePASs metric "while producing answers that cite nothing" ([Deußer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30009)). Commercial legal research tools still hallucinated 17–33% of the time in a preregistered independent study ([Magesh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362)). On a benchmark of every US state's unemployment-insurance requirements, standard RAG scored about 70%, Lexis+ AI 64% and Westlaw AI 58% (independent; [LaborBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03300)).

**Enforcement** is where retrieval stops being enough. Outcome-only scoring hides most violations: in CXBench, 55.5% of 840 runs were false passes, which is **80.1% of the runs that passed on outcome** ([CXBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25954)). IBM's ToolGuard compiled policies into Python guards run before each tool call. On policy-violating airline tasks it raised pass^10 from 0.227 to **0.500**, while appending the policy to the prompt reached only 0.273. It cannot catch omitted actions ([ToolGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16459)). Gates impose a "verifier tax". Safety mediation intercepted up to 94% of non-compliant actions, but strictly safe success stayed below 5% in most settings. Recovery after a block ranged from 21% down to near zero, and agents hallucinated user IDs to get around authentication ([Sah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19328)). Approval gates themselves leaked side effects in all six open-source agent frameworks tested (215 of 1,200 live runs), so the gate must sit outside the agent process ([Khan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14166)). Learned guard models can be "rule-blind": deleting or swapping the governing rule left their accuracy unchanged (independent; [Sadhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16852)). The resulting design is layered. Retrieval-grounded rule cards with citations serve advisory answers. Deterministic, fail-closed gates check mutating tool calls and return the rule text and the missing facts. Trace monitors cover ordering rules. A post-hoc process audit links rule cards to trace evidence. Every decision is logged with rule ID, version, input facts and outcome.

| Rule characteristic | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Gates a side-effecting action (refund, data access, payment, configuration change) | Executable binding plus pre-execution gate outside the agent |
| Numeric thresholds, fees, eligibility, tax or benefit calculation | DMN, ASP, Catala or SMT; LLM extracts facts only |
| Ordering or deadlines ("verify identity before", "notify within 72h") | Temporal monitor over the trace |
| Interpretive ("reasonable", "appropriate conduct") | Retrieval plus LLM judgement with citation and human escalation |
| Advisory question about what a rule says | Rule card retrieval with per-rule citations |
| Version- or jurisdiction-dependent | Hard metadata filter before retrieval or evaluation |

### SOPs and procedures: one canonical step graph, rendered for the model, guarded at runtime

Agents follow long procedures poorly when left to infer structure from prose. Given the full procedure in the prompt, o1-preview's exact-sequence match fell from 0.656 on short problems to 0.333 on long ones ([ProcBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03117)). Recovering step dependencies from text peaked at 0.73 F1 ([CaT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15823)). The structure should therefore be made explicit once, at ingestion, and verified. No single format wins everywhere. In FlowBench, the only benchmark that holds a workflow constant while varying its format, flowcharts gave GPT-4o the best tool-invocation F1 (75.5 vs 69.1 for text). Code-style pseudo-code hurt weaker models, and an ensemble of all three formats had the best session success (author-reported; [FlowBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14884)). Compiling prose SOPs into pseudo-code "never significantly hurts and gains up to 16.0 points where official prose underperforms". Adding a stack-machine runtime lifted one banking domain from 70.4 to 92.8, but it cost weak models 14–26 points (author-reported; [Compile, Then Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11346)). Airbnb's internal Intent-Context-Action pseudo-code raised policy-workflow accuracy from 0.57 for rich text to 0.70, and to 0.92 with chain of thought (author-reported, internal experiments only; [Airbnb ICA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10331)).

Extraction is the weak link. LLMs extract actors and actions well, but "for gateway and flow predictions, all LLMs can hardly get >0.5 F1" ([PAGED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03630)). Pipelines that work generate a typed intermediate representation, validate it structurally and by execution, repair it in a loop, and flag ambiguity for humans. Microsoft's StepFly turned real troubleshooting guides into execution DAGs and reports about 94% success with GPT-4.1 (author-reported; [StepFly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10074)). An entropy score on medical-guideline conversion rose with document complexity and so flagged documents needing clarification ([Menaka Sekar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07817)). Flowchart images need their own path, because VLMs "frequently hallucinate nonexistent connections and decision paths" ([FlowPathAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01344)).

Retrieval should treat a procedure as **one unit**, matched on its purpose and on the problems it solves rather than its step text. Microsoft's TSG Suggester kept each troubleshooting guide's section tree, attached LLM-written problem abstractions to internal nodes, and fused embeddings with entity matching. It reached 54.78% top-1, **+8.58 points over text RAG**, while captioning screenshots *hurt* by 22.64 top-5 points ([TSG Suggester](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35780)). Plain embeddings "treat procedures as unordered bags of words" and hit a generalization cliff on new contexts, while LLM-generated procedural abstractions transfer ([Kohar & Krishnan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21730)). Graph layers help execution more than recall. Across 690 skills, a hybrid lexical-plus-dense ranker found the right skill in the top 5 for 73.5% of realistic queries, while a typed workflow graph did 11.2 points worse at a matched token budget ([Kolluru & Sportsman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06196)).

Exposure follows Anthropic's Agent Skills pattern of **progressive disclosure**. A name and a description of at most 1,024 characters are always loaded, the body loads when relevant, and scripts and references load on demand ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills); [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices)). Independent evaluation shows that curation and compactness matter more than format. On SkillsBench, curated skills raised the average pass rate from 33.9% to **50.5%**. Two or three skills helped most, four or more added only 10.1 points, compact skills added 19.0 points against 0.7 for "comprehensive documentation", and self-generated skills landed **8.1–11.5 points below** the no-skill baseline ([SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670)). On 98 public software-engineering skills, 92 had no significant effect, and the harmful ones "anchor the agent on their own templates and override the task's explicit instructions" ([SWE-Skills-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15401)). Rewriting curated skills with an LLM lowered small-model success from 42.5% to as little as 29.2% ([Xu & Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03048)). Skill files are also an attack surface: 26.1% of community skills contain vulnerabilities ([Xu & Yan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12430)).

Adherence remains unsolved, so high-risk procedures need runtime enforcement. Amazon's SOP-Bench found best results ranging from 57% to 100% by domain, and newer models were not always better ([SOP-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08119)). Agents "can be easily jailbroken to overlook SOPs" ([SOPBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08669)). Vendors converge on the same answer under different names: Decagon AOPs, Intercom Fin Procedures, Sierra journeys and Salesforce Agent Script. Natural-language steps written by operations staff are combined with deterministic conditions and code for eligibility and amounts, versioned, and regression-tested with simulated conversations ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/13449439-building-fin-procedures); [Decagon](https://decagon.ai/resources/from-sops-to-agent-operating-procedures); [Salesforce Architects](https://architect.salesforce.com/docs/architect/fundamentals/guide/hybrid-reasoning-agentforce-builder-agent-script)). Intercom says plainly that natural-language instructions "guide behavior rather than guaranteeing it" (vendor docs).

The table below summarizes the three types.

| | Domain knowledge | Rules and policies | SOPs and procedures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canonical unit | SKOS concept; evidence-first topic or entity page | Rule card linked to source provision; optional executable binding | Typed procedure IR: trigger, steps, conditions, preconditions, tools, risk, escalation, tests |
| Extraction risk | Over-generated definitions; LLM-invented hierarchies | Lost cross-references; under-constrained executable rules | Missed gateways and branches; hallucinated flowchart edges |
| Index emphasis | Labels and aliases boosted; facets as filters; related-topic links | Provision-level units with breadcrumbs; citation closure; hard as-of, jurisdiction and role filters | One unit per procedure; "when to use", problem abstractions, entities; steps as sub-documents |
| Agent access | `define`, `related`, `browse`, page reads | `kb_search` with filters, `check_rules` returning candidate clauses and conditions | Catalog search, then `get_procedure(view)`, `get_step` |
| Enforcement | None needed | Pre-call gates, trace monitors, process audit | Runtime tracking the active step for high-risk procedures, enabled per model |
| Strongest evidence | Glossary expansion; Corpus2Skill scope rule | StrucChunk; temporal hard filter; ToolGuard | SkillsBench; TSG Suggester; FlowBench |

## Agents search best with a short loop over files plus a hybrid index

The agent side of a knowledge base has moved fast since 2025. The evidence now supports a fairly specific shape: a bounded search loop, a markdown file tree with a compact index, a handful of well-described tools, memory written back into the same knowledge base, and long context used as a working set rather than as the store.

### Iterative retrieval pays on multi-hop questions, but two rounds capture most of it

Agent-driven retrieval reliably beats single-shot RAG on multi-hop questions. On FRAMES, frontier models scored 0.40 without retrieval and 0.66 with a multi-step pipeline ([FRAMES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12941)). On BrowseComp-Plus, a fixed 100k-document corpus with verified evidence, GPT-5 scored 55.9% with BM25 and 70.1% with a Qwen3 embedder ([BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06600)). Tuned BM25 with deep pagination reached 83.1% ([Pi-Serini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10848)), and a "first move" that decomposes the question into clues, runs complementary searches and reranks before the loop lifted that to **90.5%** ([Question's Gambit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14412)). The gains come mostly from a good retriever, a reranker and a short loop. In a 5,000-question ablation, two retrieval iterations captured 95% of the gains of five, and fixed hybrid RRF beat rule-based adaptive routing ([arXiv 2606.21553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21553)). On BrowseComp-Plus, "moderate reranking often yields larger gains than increasing search-time reasoning" ([arXiv 2601.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14224)). A head-to-head on simpler retrieval tasks found agentic RAG used about 3.3× the input tokens and up to 3.6× the cost of a well-tuned fixed pipeline, and concluded that "neither Enhanced nor Agentic RAG is universally superior" ([arXiv 2601.07711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07711)). For a domain knowledge base, the evidence supports this loop: plan or decompose, run a hybrid search with reranking, read, and do at most one or two more rounds with a stopping rule. A retrieval-aware stagnation controller cut an average of 14 search calls per query on BrowseComp-Plus without losing accuracy ([arXiv 2608.15191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15191)).

Policy work adds one requirement: agents must re-retrieve as the situation changes. On τ-Knowledge's banking domain (about 700 interlinked documents, with tasks needing 18.6 documents and 9.5 tool calls on average), frontier agents started at 25.5% pass^1 ([τ-Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04370)). By May 2026 the leader had reached 37.4% pass^1 and 20.6% pass^4. Sierra reports that stronger agents search again as the conversation evolves and search more precisely, while weaker ones retrieve once at the start (vendor-reported; [Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-knowledge)).

### Markdown knowledge bases with one level of disclosure match production RAG

The 2025–2026 practitioner consensus, popularized by Anthropic, Manus and OpenAI Codex, is a "just-in-time" hybrid: a small always-loaded map, a navigable tree of markdown pages with descriptive names and frontmatter, and grep, glob and read tools to pull detail on demand ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). The most relevant test is Airbnb's. GPT-4.1 compiled 2,881 customer-support SOP and policy articles into about 2,700 "Wikipedia-style" pages with a running index, typed links and stable source IDs, and 96% of 3,168 audited links were supported. An agent then used a skill with hybrid search over the wiki, an article reader, and a validator that rejected any quote not found verbatim in its cited source. On 132 real cases it matched the scripted production RAG on guidance (99.2% vs 96.2%, confidence intervals overlapping) and beat it on **evidence sufficiency (83.3% vs 59.8%)**. It took about 90 seconds per case against 8–9 seconds, and its ablations were not statistically significant (vendor-run, single study; [Airbnb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14428)). Vercel found that an 8KB compressed docs index in AGENTS.md scored 100% on APIs absent from training data, against 79% for an explicitly invoked skill and 53% for a skill left to the agent's discretion, because there was "no decision point" about whether to look things up (vendor-run, single study; [Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals)).

Hierarchy depth has a clear limit. On ∞Bench, a flat one-level skill pack helped weak harnesses and was neutral for a harness that already greps. A two-level hierarchy "never helps and sometimes breaks accuracy", dropping one harness from 0.913 to 0.640. Across 20 books, raw navigation collapsed while flat retrieval held up at about half the tokens. The authors' summary: "progressive disclosure buys context, not intelligence" ([arXiv 2607.17598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17598)). Always-loaded instruction files carry their own risk. Context files "do not generally improve task success rates, while increasing inference cost by over 20%" ([arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)). With a deliberately stale file, **90.1%** of agent choices followed the file over the code ([arXiv 2610.30571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30571)), and instruction files more than triple over their lifetime ([arXiv 2608.11095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11095)). The always-loaded index must therefore be small, generated, and linted. Knowledge content belongs behind tools. llms.txt, often proposed for exactly this purpose, has no measured benefit: 97% of domains with the file recorded no requests for it, and adversarial manifest content steered outputs in 21 of 24 trials ([arXiv 2610.38361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38361)).

Grep over raw text is a serious retriever in its own right. Agents using only grep, file reads and shell scripts outperformed strong sparse, dense and reranking baselines on several BRIGHT and BEIR sets ([DCI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05242)). Raw grep becomes unstable as corpora grow, but retrieval that first pulls candidates into a local workspace, which the agent then greps, stayed effective from 100K to 10M documents ([Dr-DCI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14885)). Harness effects are as large as retriever effects; the same model scored 93.1 and 76.7 in different harnesses ([arXiv 2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)).

### Five consolidated tools beat dozens of connector-specific ones

Commercial knowledge-base MCP servers converge on a search-then-fetch pair. Glean's managed toolset centers on `search` and `read_document`, and Glean advises against "one server with all tools enabled" because it lowers tool-selection accuracy ([Glean](https://docs.glean.com/administration/platform/mcp/create-mcp-servers)). Notion exposes search, fetch and backlinks ([Notion](https://developers.notion.com/guides/mcp/tools-search)). Dropbox replaced five to ten per-app retrieval tools with one "super tool" backed by its universal index. One MCP-based query had taken up to 45 seconds against seconds on the raw index ([Dropbox](https://dropbox.tech/machine-learning/vp-josh-clemm-knowledge-graphs-mcp-and-dspy-dash)). Shopify found tool boundaries blur past about 20 tools and break past 50, and it moved instructions out of the system prompt and into tool results ("just-in-time instructions"), which also preserves the prompt cache ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)).

Tool details measurably change outcomes. Of 856 tools on 103 MCP servers, 97.1% had at least one description defect, and augmenting descriptions raised median success by 5.85 points but increased steps by 67% ([arXiv 2602.14878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14878)). Description edits alone can shift a tool's usage more than tenfold when it competes with alternatives ([arXiv 2505.18135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18135)). Delivering results in a file instead of inline dropped one grep harness from 93.1 to 55.2 ([arXiv 2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)). An RL-trained search agent degraded sharply at 40 results per search against 10–20 (vendor-run; [KARL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05218)), which favors small pages with cheap pagination over large top-k dumps. Anthropic's guidance adds concise and detailed response formats, human-readable identifiers and actionable errors ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents)). Citation validity is best enforced in code: Airbnb rejected any snippet that was not a substring of its cited article, and 99.7% of 1,769 snippets came out byte-identical (vendor-reported; [Airbnb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14428)).

### Agent memory is a knowledge base written by agents, and simple baselines win

Memory products are knowledge bases that agents write. Their benchmark evidence is weak and contested. On the vendor-independent MemoryAgentBench, BM25 scored 60.5 on accurate retrieval against 37.5 for Zep, 34.3 for MemGPT and 32.6 for Mem0, and no system covered all four competencies ([MemoryAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257)). On derived state, every memory system fell behind BM25 ([MemDerive](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25373)). The popular LoCoMo benchmark fits in context, its answer key is partly wrong, and an independent critique found its common judge setup accepted 62.8% of deliberately wrong answers ([D'Erme](https://dev.to/gde03/the-ai-memory-benchmark-everyone-quotes-forbids-saying-i-dont-know-o1n)). Vendors dispute one another's numbers: Zep says Mem0 misconfigured Zep in its comparison, Mem0 reportedly answers that Zep's headline figure counted an extra question category, and Mem0's latest 92.5 comes from its managed platform with "proprietary optimizations" (vendor-reported; [Zep](https://www.getzep.com/blog/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/); [Mem0](https://docs.mem0.ai/core-concepts/memory-evaluation)). Letta's plain file-system agent scored 74.0% on LoCoMo, which it attributes to tool-use skill rather than memory mechanism (competitor-reported; [Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory)). The durable finding is consolidation: distilling episodes into reusable playbooks and workflows improves agents. Agent Workflow Memory gained 24.6% and 51.1% relative success on Mind2Web and WebArena ([AWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429)), and ACE's evolving playbooks gained 10.6% on agent tasks ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)). That output is exactly SOP and rule content. Memory should therefore be modeled as write-back into the knowledge base: learned facts and gotchas become `status: proposed` pages with their source trajectories, and a periodic consolidation pass merges them into procedures and rules after review. SkillsBench's finding that self-written skills hurt is the reason the review gate is not optional.

### Long context is a working set, not the store

Long context often matches retrieval when the whole knowledge base fits. Self-Route found long-context answers beat RAG on average (49.70 vs 37.33 for Gemini-1.5-Pro), with identical predictions on about 63% of queries, and routing by self-reflection used 38–61% of the long-context tokens at comparable accuracy ([Self-Route](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833)). Anthropic suggests skipping RAG entirely below about 200k tokens ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). Accuracy decays with competition, though, not with length. Neutral filler cost nothing up to 32K tokens, but evidence-format distractors dropped accuracy to 0.16–0.33, and a single rival value multiplied loss **9–110×** ([arXiv 2611.04169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169)). With minimal lexical overlap, GPT-4o fell from 99.3% to 69.7% at 32K ([NoLiMa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)). A policy knowledge base full of near-identical clauses and superseded versions is precisely that worst case. Caching changes the economics of the always-loaded part: cached reads cost 0.1× base input, or 0.05× on the newest Claude models ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). The working design always loads a cached prefix (index, core definitions, top rules), retrieves pages just in time, and reads whole documents when a question needs one. Retrieval surfaces *any* relevant chunk far more reliably than *all* required chunks, and chunking strips the identity information that some questions depend on ([arXiv 2610.26951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26951)). It also exposes only the effective version of each policy by default.

## Stale, duplicated and conflicting content causes more policy hallucinations than weak retrieval

Deployed systems rarely fail because nothing relevant was retrieved. They fail because something wrong, outdated or contradictory was retrieved and believed. Salesforce found that apparent hallucinations often traced to an old, unlinked, rarely updated page that contradicted maintained articles. After cleaning up duplicates and outdated content, its SDR agent's "I don't know" rate fell from 30% to under 10% (vendor-reported; [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/first-year-agentforce-customer-zero/)). Air Canada's chatbot promised a retroactive bereavement refund that contradicted the linked policy page. The tribunal rejected the argument that the bot was a "separate legal entity" and held the airline liable ([Manatt on Moffatt v. Air Canada](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/ai-gone-wild-airline-has-to-honor-a-refund-policy)). NYC's MyCity bot, grounded on more than 2,000 official pages, still told businesses they could take workers' tips ([The Markup](https://themarkup.org/news/2024/03/29/nycs-ai-chatbot-tells-businesses-to-break-the-law)). Lifecycle management is therefore a core accuracy feature, not housekeeping.

### Freshness: superseded content must be filtered out, not ranked down

Incremental ingestion is a solved engineering problem. Content hashes plus a record manager re-process only added, changed and deleted documents ([LangChain](https://reference.langchain.com/python/langchain-core/indexing/api/index); [AWS Bedrock](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/kb-data-source-sync-ingest.html)). Notion stores separate text and metadata hashes per span, which cut data volume by about 70%, and patches permission changes without re-embedding ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/two-years-of-vector-search-at-notion)). Temporal validity, by contrast, is not solved. In HoH, built from monthly Wikipedia diffs, adding one outdated passage dropped Llama-3.1-70B from 89.12 to 72.95, with 10.05% harmful answers. Smaller models fell much further, and outdated passages were retrieved slightly *more* often than current ones. Gaussian time decay cut outdated hits but also cut relevant hits from about 0.87 to 0.70 ([HoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04800)). Across 317 verified knowledge reversals in medicine, law, software and platform policy, outdated evidence flipped 30–37% of answers that were correct without retrieval, and **66–75%** when the model was told to follow the documents. Telling models *when the old evidence stops applying* let larger models switch almost perfectly, while a recency-aware reranker cut poisoning by only 4.6–10.0 points, and only when dates were accurate ([Stale-Document Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31342)).

This is a deliberate break from human knowledge-management practice. KCS argues that archiving old articles "treats the symptoms of findability, not the cause" ([KCS v6](https://library.serviceinnovation.org/KCS/KCS_v6/KCS_v6_Practices_Guide/030/040/010/044)). For LLM consumers, a superseded passage in the top-k is actively harmful. Agent knowledge bases should therefore exclude superseded and expired content by a hard validity filter, keep history addressable only for explicit "as-of" queries, and surface "superseded on date X by unit Y" text to the generator. Graphiti's bi-temporal model, which invalidates facts rather than deleting them, is the reference pattern ([Graphiti](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti)). Review intervals should scale with volatility, as in Guru's per-card verification intervals and CRAG's dynamism classes ([Guru](https://help.getguru.com/docs/verifying-and-unverifying-cards); [CRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04744)). ACL freshness is a separate service-level target. Azure notes that permission changes inherited from site or folder scope "require an explicit refresh" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/search/search-document-level-access-overview)). Embedding upgrades can be bridged by a learned adapter that reportedly recovers 95–99% of re-embedding recall at under 1% of the cost while a blue/green rebuild runs (author-reported; [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471)).

### Versions are chains, duplicates are collapsed, paraphrases go to review

Exact duplicates fall to normalized hashes (SHA-256; LangChain itself warns its default SHA-1 is not collision-resistant). Near-duplicates need MinHash-LSH or SimHash. Google's web-scale study found 64-bit SimHash at k=3 reached precision and recall of only about 0.75, so thresholds must be tuned on local labelled pairs ([Manku et al.](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/33026.pdf)). Embedding-cluster "semantic duplicates" should be flagged for review rather than deleted, because paraphrased policies can differ in one critical clause. Versions of the same document must not be collapsed as duplicates. Modeling version chains and routing by intent (version-specific vs change-tracking) reached 90% accuracy against 58% for naive RAG on a small versioned-documentation benchmark (author-reported; [VersionRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08109)). Elastic fixed its support assistant's near-duplicate article versions by collapsing on a canonical slug and boosting an extracted version field ([Elastic](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/elser-rag-search-for-relevance)). Canonical selection is a governance rule: designated system of record first, then approval state, then latest effective date, then reuse. Non-canonical copies keep a pointer so that citations resolve to the authoritative copy.

### Conflicts belong in the data layer, and poisoning is stopped at the write path

LLMs handle conflicting context badly. They adopted wrong retrieved content over their own correct prior **more than 60% of the time** ([ClashEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198)). They rarely surfaced contradictions between equally trustworthy passages ([WikiContradict](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13805)). With naturally misleading retrievals, every RAG system tested did worse than its zero-shot baseline ([RAGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16101)). Conflicts must therefore be caught before generation. Offline contradiction audits are affordable as a cascade: a cheap classifier first, then an LLM only on uncertain pairs, which cut API cost by 62% at 90.8% detection accuracy (author-reported; [ConflictRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17301)). Candidate pairs need BM25 as well as embeddings, because dense retrievers "stay near chance" at separating a statement from its negation ([CoverageBench-Negate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37162)). Not every conflict is an error. Distinguishing legitimate evolution from attack and from unresolved disagreement improved attribution and halved error under coordinated attack (author-reported; [EvoTrustRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07933)). A regional policy variant is a scoping fact to record, not a contradiction to delete.

Poisoning is practical and hard to detect from content. Five crafted passages per target question in corpora of millions gave **90–99% attack success**, and duplicate filtering had no effect ([PoisonedRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07867)). In a policy-aware RAG system, one injected rule (a 1.6% poisoning rate) corrupted 85% of contexts ([Solanki et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04379)). Two 2026 critiques undercut much of the published defense literature. Deleting a shortcut prefix that PoisonedRAG's code adds to every poison dropped one content filter's AUROC from 0.953 to 0.758, and 12 of 21 defense papers reported no false-positive rate at their operating threshold ([Ranking Is Not Deciding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33849)). A coordinated set of ten unremarkable documents seized all ten top-k slots. The authors proved that no ingestion-time statistic can separate this from legitimate niche uploads: the best classifier caught 4.2% at 1% FPR, while a retrieval-time detector watching *query demand* caught 100% ([Coverage Is Not Containment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16044)). The robust controls are therefore about who can write, not what the text looks like: allow-listed sources, signed and owner-approved policy documents, quarantine for user-contributed or external content, demand-anomaly monitoring at retrieval time, and isolate-then-aggregate generation for high-stakes rule answers ([RobustRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15556)).

### Permissions are pre-filters on every chunk, with fail-closed sync

The consensus across vendors and a Microsoft Research position paper is deterministic enforcement. Every retrieved chunk must be authorized for every principal in the interaction, enforced inside the retrieval engine, never by prompting. The paper calls prompt-based defenses "fundamentally probabilistic" ([Bhatt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14608)). ACLs must be projected onto each chunk ("without this projection, chunk-level references aren't filtered"; [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/search/search-document-level-access-overview)). Principals should be stored as stable IDs with inheritance resolved at ingest, and post-retrieval filtering is "an anti-pattern" ([Microsoft ISE](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/ise/?p=16628)). Atlassian Rovo hides a connector's results entirely when its permission sync is stale ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/organization-administration/docs/how-connector-permissions-are-kept-in-sync/)). AWS calls Bedrock's metadata filtering only a "baseline" and recommends checking each retrieved chunk against the source's authorization ([AWS Security Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/authorizing-access-to-data-with-rag-implementations)). Retrieval opens leakage channels beyond the ACL. A membership-inference attack needs about 30 natural queries and costs under **$0.02 per document** ([Naseh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00306)). Embedding inversion recovered 92% of 32-token inputs exactly, which an independent reproduction confirmed under ideal conditions, so vectors are as sensitive as text ([Vec2Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06816); [reproduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07700)). EchoLeak showed what happens when untrusted inbound content shares a context with privileged data: a crafted email exfiltrated Copilot data with zero clicks (CVSS 9.3; [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/zero-click-ai-vulnerability-exposes.html)). PII redaction tools help but disclaim completeness ([Presidio](https://presidio.dataprivacystack.org/)). An immutable retrieval log answers "what did the agent read" and supports forensics ([OWASP LLM08](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm082025-vector-and-embedding-weaknesses/)). It should record the query, the principals, the chunk IDs with version hashes, the ACL decision, the citations and the model used.

### Evaluation: golden sets, claim-level diagnostics, calibrated judges and production gap signals

No single metric suffices, so evaluation works in layers. A versioned golden set measures retrieval with gold unit IDs. Claim-level diagnostics separate retrieval misses from generator errors. In its authors' meta-evaluation, RAGChecker correlated with human preference at 61.93 against 48.31 for RAGAS answer similarity, with a human ceiling of 70.09 (author-run; [RAGChecker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08067)). ARES calibrates LLM judges with prediction-powered inference on about 150–300 human labels, giving confidence intervals at 78% fewer annotations ([ARES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09476)). LLM judges agree with humans at the system level, but query-term stuffing fools their relevance labels ([Alaofi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17969)), and NIST's Soboroff argues against replacing human judgments for TREC-style evaluation ([Soboroff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15133)). Practitioners report the same need for calibration. Shopify raised judge–human Cohen's κ from 0.02 to 0.61 against a human–human baseline of 0.69 ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)), and Dropbox cut judge–human disagreement from 8% ([Dropbox](https://dropbox.tech/machine-learning/vp-josh-clemm-knowledge-graphs-mcp-and-dspy-dash)). The most actionable production signal is "sufficient context". Models with insufficient context "often output incorrect answers instead of abstaining", and classifying production queries this way turns failures into a content-gap backlog ([Joren et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037)). For actions, process compliance must be checked alongside outcomes, given CXBench's 80% false-pass rate. Dify's golden questions, which go "Stale" when their expected chunk disappears and open bad cases automatically when production misses, are the best open implementation of this loop ([Dify](https://github.com/langgenius/dify-docs/blob/main/en/cloud/use-dify/knowledge-base/test-and-tune.mdx)).

### Curation: KCS supplies the operating model, agents supply the signals

Knowledge-Centered Service (KCS v6) supplies a tested human operating model. Its Solve Loop (capture, structure, reuse, improve) runs alongside an Evolve Loop (content health, process integration, performance assessment). A six-item content standard covers unique, complete, clear, title, valid links and correct metadata, and is sampled monthly, at "a few hours per reviewer each month" in mature programs. Its targets favor reuse accuracy over reuse rate and link accuracy above 90% ([KCS v6](https://library.serviceinnovation.org/KCS/KCS_v6/KCS_v6_Practices_Guide/030/040/010/065)). Agents generate the signals KCS needs automatically. "Reuse" becomes a unit cited in an accepted, non-escalated answer. "Flag it or fix it" becomes agent and user feedback opening tickets for the owner. "Content gap" becomes clusters of insufficient-context queries. Commercial tools already close this loop under human approval. Intercom compares failed Fin answers with successful human replies, runs weekly duplicate and contradiction scans, and tracks "involved" against "resolved" counts per content item. Zendesk drafts articles from 90 days of solved tickets labelled "AI-generated" ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-suggestions-to-improve-fin); [Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/zendesk-insights/innovation/fuel-your-ai-with-a-knowledge-base-built-in-days-not-months/)). The bottleneck is upstream human discipline. In a Gartner survey, 61% of service leaders had a content-update backlog ([CDO Magazine](https://www.cdomagazine.tech/aiml/85-of-customer-service-leaders-to-explore-genai-solutions-by-2025-gartner-survey)), and ServiceNow's AI team found that tickets closed with "Fixed it" starve knowledge generation ([ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/community/ceg-ai-coe-articles/why-your-itsm-agents-are-closing-tickets-fast-and-teaching-your/ta-p/3484316)).

## Platforms have converged on one pattern, and almost every outcome number is self-measured

### Enterprise search assistants differ in freshness, ACL strictness and billing, not retrieval technique

Every enterprise knowledge assistant uses the same three layers. Connectors crawl content, ACLs and identities into a hybrid lexical-plus-vector index. An activity or people graph adds ranking signals. Permissions are enforced again at query time. In 2025–26 each vendor also added a federated path that fetches live with the user's own token for content that must not be copied. Glean stores entities and relations with timestamps, ACLs, confidence and provenance, and runs indexed, real-time and read/write connector modes ([Glean](https://www.glean.com/blog/knowledge-graph-agentic-engine); [Glean docs](https://docs.glean.com/connectors/native/onedrive/about)). Microsoft 365 Copilot's semantic index cannot be disabled. It indexes mailbox content near real-time but new shared SharePoint documents only daily, and synced connectors are semantically indexed on title and content only ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftsearch/semantic-index-for-copilot)). Google states that federation "may lower quality" relative to ingestion, and that incremental sync misses deletions and identity changes, so a periodic full sync is needed ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connectors/introduction-to-connectors-and-data-stores)). Amazon Q Business is closed to new customers ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/amazonq/latest/qbusiness-ug/connector-concepts.md)). Bedrock Knowledge Bases has no native source-ACL sync and returns the full page or table (up to 10,000 characters) when a markdown table chunk matches ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/kb-chunking.html)). Guru positions itself as a verified-knowledge layer. Cards carry verifiers and expiry intervals, daily AI agents un-verify stale or duplicate cards, and downstream agents can be restricted to verified content ([Guru](https://help.getguru.com/docs/verifying-and-unverifying-cards)).

Procedures are authored as natural-language steps with deterministic guards and tested with simulations. Its Agent Script gates actions out of the LLM's tool list until conditions hold and runs authentication checks before reasoning ([Salesforce Architects](https://architect.salesforce.com/docs/architect/fundamentals/guide/hybrid-reasoning-agentforce-builder-agent-script)). Fin Procedures allow conditions in natural language or Python, and Intercom tells customers to keep simple FAQs as articles, not procedures ([Fin](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976187-fin-procedures-explained)). Consolidation is rapid. ServiceNow closed its acquisition of Moveworks in December 2025 ([ServiceNow](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2025/ServiceNow-completes-acquisition-of-Moveworks/default.aspx)), Salesforce agreed to buy Fin for about $3.6B in June 2026 ([Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/salesforce-to-acquire-fin-ai-formerly-intercom-for-3-6-billion/)), and Dashworks shut down three months after HubSpot acquired it, with a single export window ([Vantagepoint](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/hs/what-happened-to-dashworks-ai)).

### Open-source stacks match the commercial feature set except free source-ACL sync

The open-source field has converged on the same feature set: connectors with scheduled sync and deletion pruning, title-path and parent-child chunking, LLM enrichment, hybrid retrieval with reranking and metadata filters, citations, and a retrieval-test screen. **RAGFlow** (Apache-2.0) is the feature benchmark. It ships document-type templates (Manual, Laws, Q&A, Table), a title chunker that keeps full heading paths and is recommended for "legal provisions, regulations, contracts", an extractor for keywords, questions and metadata, and since August 2026 "Knowledge Compilation" into wikis, trees, graphs and skills. Its permissions, however, are only "Only me" or "Team", with no per-document ACL from source systems ([RAGFlow](https://github.com/infiniflow/ragflow/blob/main/docs/guides/dataset/configuration.md); [RAGFlow permissions](https://github.com/infiniflow/ragflow/blob/main/docs/guides/team/resource_sharing/resource_sharing_and_permissions.md)). **Dify** has the best-documented evaluation loop but a non-OSI license, and its chunk mode cannot be changed once a knowledge base is created ([Dify](https://github.com/langgenius/dify-docs/blob/main/en/self-host/use-dify/knowledge/create-knowledge/chunking-and-cleaning-text.mdx)). **Onyx** (MIT core) has the cleanest sync model: indexing every 30 minutes, pruning deleted documents every 7 days, and a separate permission sync. That permission sync requires the paid Enterprise Edition ([Onyx](https://docs.onyx.app/admins/connectors/overview)). **Open WebUI** (custom license) exposes knowledge bases to agents as tools and as a read-only shell (`ls`, `grep`, `cat -n`) that "respects the same access control" ([Open WebUI](https://github.com/open-webui/docs/blob/main/docs/features/workspace/knowledge.mdx)). Among graph and memory engines, Graphiti (Apache-2.0) offers bi-temporal facts with provenance to source episodes, and LightRAG (MIT) added deletion with automatic graph regeneration. Mem0's open SDK does not reproduce its published scores, which come from the managed platform ([Mem0](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0)). PageIndex's "vectorless" FinanceBench 98.7% claim is unreplicated (vendor-reported; [PageIndex](https://github.com/VectifyAI/PageIndex)). Churn is high: Verba is archived, Quivr and SurfSense pivoted, R2R has been dormant since November 2025, and LlamaCloud Index v1 is deprecated ([Verba](https://github.com/weaviate/Verba); [Quivr](https://github.com/QuivrHQ/quivr); [R2R](https://github.com/SciPhi-AI/R2R/blob/main/py/README.md)). A toolkit should own its data model and treat these projects as adapters.

Search engines all offer native hybrid retrieval now, so the choice turns on license and operations. Postgres with pgvector (PostgreSQL License) covers small and medium knowledge bases. ParadeDB adds true BM25 but is AGPL. OpenSearch (Apache-2.0) is the safest full-featured choice, with RRF and per-subquery weights. Qdrant, Milvus and LanceDB (all Apache-2.0) suit vector-first stacks with built-in sparse retrieval. Elasticsearch's triple license (AGPLv3, SSPL or ELv2) and the enterprise carve-outs in Weaviate and Meilisearch need license review for embedding ([licenses as of Nov 2026](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch); [Elasticsearch](https://github.com/elastic/elasticsearch/blob/main/LICENSE.txt); [ParadeDB](https://github.com/paradedb/paradedb)). Permissive end-to-end source-ACL sync for Confluence, SharePoint, Drive and Slack does not exist in open source. Onyx's is paid, Elastic's connectors are ELv2, and Airbyte is ELv2 ([Elastic connectors](https://github.com/elastic/connectors); [Airbyte](https://github.com/airbytehq/airbyte)). That gap is the clearest opening for an open toolkit.

| UK DBT Copilot pilot | 1,000 licences | Time savings on writing, "no evidence" of productivity gains ([Civil Service World](https://www.civilserviceworld.com/news/article/dbt-trial-finds-copilot-saves-staff-time-but-doesnt-improve-productivity)) | Independent |

The failures are just as consistent. Microsoft makes "remediate oversharing" the first step of every Copilot deployment ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/plan/secure-govern-copilot-foundational-deployment-guidance)), and 66% of surveyed decision-makers had delayed or cancelled Copilot over SharePoint exposure fears (vendor survey; [CoreView](https://www.coreview.com/news/66-percent-of-enterprises-delay-microsoft-copilot)). The UK trial noted that agents struggled to identify which documents they had used, which limits traceability. Cursor's support bot invented a "one device per subscription" policy ([CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/contact-center/a-contact-center-chatbot-invents-company-policies-now-customers-want-out/)). Klarna's CEO conceded its automation push "went too far" and resumed hiring human agents ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/klarna-dials-back-its-ai-customer-service-strategy-now-its-hiring-humans-again/)). Five practical lessons recur across these sources. Fix permissions and content duplication before launch. Keep FAQs as content and procedures as tested executable artifacts. Prefer one broad search tool to many per-source tools. Calibrate LLM judges against human labels before trusting them. Treat knowledge-base linting (stale, duplicate, contradictory, underspecified) as a product feature, as Uber, DoorDash and Elastic each did ([DoorDash via ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/llm-based-dasher-support-automation-with-rag-and-quality-controls)).

## Blueprint: a knowledge-unit compiler behind a small tool surface, seven lifecycle jobs and an evaluation gate

The recommended architecture is a compiler, not a chat application. Connectors feed an immutable evidence store of converted sources. A canonical document tree with stable node IDs sits on that store. Typed knowledge units are compiled from the tree. Several synchronized views are built from the units: a markdown file tree, a hybrid index, a link graph and executable bindings. Agents reach them through a small tool surface. An enforcement gate and a set of scheduled lifecycle jobs surround the whole. Every element below has at least one controlled or deployment result behind it, but the assembly has not been evaluated end to end. It should be built in the order given, with each phase gated by the team's own measurements.

### Data model: one envelope, six unit types

Every unit shares an envelope that carries identity, provenance, applicability and governance. Type-specific bodies sit inside it. The envelope fields come from the lifecycle evidence (validity, authority, ACL and hash fields), from the platforms (owner, verification, audience, usage statistics) and from established content standards (KCS, DITA, SKOS), so that the format is explainable rather than invented.

| Field group | Fields | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | `unit_id`, `type`, `title`, `heading_path`, `version`, `canonical_ref` | Stable citation target; dedup to the authoritative copy ([Elastic](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/elser-rag-search-for-relevance)) |
| Provenance | `source_refs[]` (doc_id, version, char or page span), `lineage` (parser, extractor, prompt and model versions), `embedding_model_version` | Verbatim quoting, regeneration, audit ([Airbnb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14428)) |
| Applicability | `status` (proposed, draft, validated, superseded, retired), `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `supersedes`, `superseded_by`, `jurisdiction`, `audience`, `role`, `product` | Hard filters; stale content flips answers ([Stale-Document Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31342); [Prior et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23497)) |
| Governance | `owner`, `verifier`, `authority_tier` (1 = system of record or approved policy … 4 = external or user content), `review_due`, `last_verified_at` | Canonical selection, review queues, poisoning control ([Guru](https://help.getguru.com/docs/verifying-and-unverifying-cards); [KCS](https://library.serviceinnovation.org/KCS/KCS_v6/KCS_v6_Practices_Guide/030/040/010/065)) |
| Access | `acl.allow[]`, `acl.deny[]` (stable principal IDs, inheritance resolved), `acl.synced_at`, `sensitivity_label`, `pii_flags` | Pre-filter enforcement, fail closed ([Microsoft ISE](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/ise/?p=16628)) |
| Change detection | `hashes{text, meta, acl}` | Incremental re-embedding, metadata-only ACL patches, golden-question staleness ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/two-years-of-vector-search-at-notion)) |
| Retrieval aids (index-side only) | `summary`, `keywords[]`, `generated_questions[]`, `entities[]`, `facets{}` | Enrichment that helps finding but is kept out of reader context ([Zerhoudi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29645)) |
| Usage | `involved_count`, `resolved_count`, `cited_count`, `last_cited_at` | KCS-style reuse and gap signals ([Intercom](https://intercom.com/help/en/articles/9790492-how-to-improve-fin-s-resolution-rate)) |

The six unit types carry these bodies. A **concept** holds SKOS labels (preferred, alternate, hidden), a definition with span, a scope note, broader, narrower and related links, and anchor-vocabulary mappings. A **page** (topic or entity) holds atomic claims, each with a verbatim span, plus a rendered narrative and typed links. A **rule card** holds rule text, modality, subject, conditions, thresholds, exceptions, defined terms, resolved cross-references, an extraction-confidence score and zero or more `bindings[]`. Each binding has a language (Cedar, Rego, DMN, ASP), a test suite and a sign-off flag. A **procedure** holds `when_to_use` (at most 1,024 characters), exclusions, positive and negative trigger examples, required inputs, preconditions and `steps[]`. Each step has a type (instruction, condition, tool call, sub-procedure, handoff, wait, end), text, an optional tool and argument schema, `next[]`, a risk level and an approval flag. Procedures also carry escalation, success criteria, problem abstractions, generated branch-coverage tests and ambiguity flags. An **article or FAQ** holds KCS fields (issue in the requester's words, environment, resolution, cause) or a question-answer pair. A **table record** holds a caption, headers, a generated description and a pointer to the typed table.

### Views and indexes

Each view serves a distinct access pattern, and all are regenerated from the units, so none is a second source of truth.

| View | Built from | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| Markdown file tree (`kb/index.md` ≤ about 8–10KB, `kb/<domain>/<unit>.md` with YAML frontmatter, `raw/` with anchors, `log.md`) | Units and raw sources | Always-loaded map, grep, read, verbatim citations ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals); [Karpathy](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)) |
| Lexical index | Breadcrumb + body + keywords + aliases; field boosts on title, identifiers, altLabels | Codes, acronyms, IDs, negations ([LIMIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21038)) |
| Dense index | Breadcrumb + summary + body; separate question vectors pointing to parent units | Paraphrase and question-style queries ([QuOTE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10976)) |
| Section tree | Canonical document tree | Small-to-big expansion, `browse` ([NavTree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06902)) |
| Term dictionary | Concept units | Query expansion and `define` ([BMQExpander](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11784)) |
| Link graph | Typed links (cites, defines, exception_of, overrides, supersedes, subprocedure_of, related) | Citation closure, effective-version resolution, `related` via PageRank ([RegOps-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29742)) |
| Executable bindings | Rule cards and procedures | Pre-call gates and procedure runtime ([ToolGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16459)) |

Retrieval defaults are hybrid lexical and dense with fusion weights tuned on the golden set, a domain-validated cross-encoder reranker, dedup by canonical reference, hard pre-filters on ACL, `status` and validity window ("now" unless an as-of date is given), and expansion of each hit to its parent section and its citation closure. Postgres with pgvector and full-text search is the single-node reference backend, and OpenSearch is the scale-out backend.

### Agent tools

The tool surface stays small and stable so that the prompt cache survives and tool selection stays accurate. All tools apply the caller's identity, intersected across every participant in the session.

| Tool | Returns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `kb_search(query, filters{type, domain, jurisdiction, as_of, status}, mode, limit=10, cursor, format)` | Ranked snippets with unit ID, title, heading path, effective date, highlights | Default `status=effective`; concise or detailed format; small pages with pagination |
| `kb_read(unit_id or path, section?, range?)` | Markdown with line anchors | Whole sections for policies and procedures |
| `kb_grep(pattern, scope)` | Matching lines with anchors | Exact identifiers; workspace-scoped at large scale ([Dr-DCI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14885)) |
| `kb_related(unit_id, relation?)` and `define(term)` | Typed neighbours; concept entry | Related topics, citation closure, glossary |
| `find_procedure(task)` → `get_procedure(id, view=skill, mermaid or pseudo)` | Catalog entries, then the rendered procedure | Progressive disclosure; one level only ([arXiv 2607.17598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17598)) |
| `check_rules(intent, facts{})` | Candidate rule cards plus binding verdict (allow, deny with rule text and missing facts, escalate) | Advisory; the binding gate on mutating tools is separate and mandatory |
| `kb_propose(content, sources, trajectory_id)` | A `status: proposed` unit | Memory write-back behind review ([SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670)) |

The same semantics should ship as an MCP server, as a CLI, and as an Agent Skills folder export, so that file-native agents and tool-calling agents see one knowledge base. The agent policy that ships alongside is short. Load the index, make a first decomposed search, do at most two or three search-and-read rounds with a stopping rule, re-search when the situation changes, cite raw anchors that code then validates, and abstain with a gap report when evidence is insufficient.

### Lifecycle jobs

| Job | Trigger and cadence | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Content sync | Webhooks where available; delta every ~30 min; full reconciliation weekly with a deletion safeguard | New, changed and deleted units; hash-skipped no-ops ([Onyx](https://docs.onyx.app/admins/connectors/overview); [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/amazonq/latest/qbusiness-ug/connector-concepts.md)) |
| Permission sync | Separate job, ~hourly, plus change feeds; fail closed past a freshness threshold | Metadata-only ACL patches; ACL-staleness metric ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/organization-administration/docs/how-connector-permissions-are-kept-in-sync/)) |
| Write-time gates | Every new or changed unit | SHA-256 and MinHash dedup, version-chain detection, PII and hidden-text scan, required validity metadata, source allow-list, contradiction check against top-k neighbours, quarantine of external content |
| Compilation | After ingest | Rule cards, procedure IRs, concept candidates, pages; judge-and-repair loops; extraction confidence and ambiguity flags to review queues ([De Jure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02276); [PAGED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03630)) |
| Lint | Nightly | Expired `review_due`, orphan concepts, dangling links, duplicate and contradictory units, stale golden questions ([LLM-Wiki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480)) |
| Gap mining and consolidation | Weekly | Clusters of insufficient-context queries, failed conversations against human resolutions, proposed units merged into procedures and rules; all writes by human approval ([Joren et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037); [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-suggestions-to-improve-fin)) |
| Owner review | Per volatility class (static ~12 months, policies on their own cycle, operational facts days to weeks or served live) | Re-verified or superseded units, with history kept |

### Evaluation

| Layer | Metric | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval | Recall@k and nDCG on gold unit and rule IDs; section coverage for synthesis questions | Every index, model or configuration change |
| Answers | Claim-level precision and recall, groundedness, citation validity, correct abstention | Every change, with an LLM judge |
| Judge calibration | Prediction-powered inference on 150–300 fresh human labels; κ against human labels | Weekly ([ARES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09476)) |
| Actions | pass^k, completion under policy, process false-pass rate | Every procedure or binding change, via simulations ([CXBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25954)) |
| Robustness | Temporal probes (old and new version both present), contradiction probes, adversarial users, poisoning drills | Monthly ([HoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04800); [τ-break](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09600)) |
| Production | Insufficient-context rate, abstention, involved vs resolved per unit, % past review date, ACL-sync lag, demand anomalies | Continuous dashboards |
| Content | KCS six-item sample per domain | Monthly |

The golden set should mix factoid, procedure-lookup, policy-applicability (with as-of dates and jurisdictions), overview, keyword or ID, and insufficient-evidence questions. It should be generated with controlled question-type distributions and verified by humans, because author-written queries overstated skill-retrieval hit rates by up to 44 points ([Kolluru & Sportsman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06196)). Each procedure and skill should also get a paired with-and-without test, since most public skills had no measurable effect and some were harmful ([SWE-Skills-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15401)).

### Build order

| Phase | Build | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Evidence and baseline | Connectors with content and ACL sync, canonical tree, structure-led chunks, breadcrumb prefix, hybrid index, reranker, `kb_search`/`kb_read`/`kb_grep`, retrieval log, golden set of a few hundred questions | Baseline recall and answer metrics recorded; ACL pre-filter verified fail-closed |
| 2. Applicability | Envelope fields, version chains, hard validity and jurisdiction filters, dedup gates, canonical selection, owners and review dates | Temporal probes pass; no superseded unit in default top-k |
| 3. Typed units | Concept table and `define`, rule cards with citation closure, procedure IR with SKILL.md and Mermaid renderers, FAQ and table records | Rule-ID recall and procedure top-5 retrieval beat phase 1 on the golden set |
| 4. Enforcement | Executable bindings for action-gating and numeric rules via verifier loops; out-of-process gate with three outcomes; procedure runtime enabled per model after a discipline check | pass^k and process false-pass rate improve on simulations without collapsing task success |
| 5. Navigation and synthesis | Markdown tree and index, facet taxonomy and related links where coherence passes, evidence-first pages for high-traffic concepts, per-chunk questions and summaries if they beat the prefix | Each layer kept only if it beats phase 3 on accuracy or tokens at acceptable latency |
| 6. Closed loop | Lint, contradiction audits, gap mining, `kb_propose` with consolidation, PPI judge audits | Gap backlog shrinking; insufficient-context rate falling |

Several defaults should be avoided because the evidence is against them: semantic, LLM or late chunking as the default; chunk overlap; RAPTOR or GraphRAG as the backbone; propositions as the primary index; dozens of per-source tools; multi-level skill hierarchies; large always-loaded instruction files; policy-in-prompt as the only control on actions; recency decay in place of validity filters; post-retrieval ACL filtering; and auto-published, model-written skills or pages.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the central problem of an agent knowledge base from *similarity* to *applicability*. Retrievers are now good enough that the remaining failures come mainly from what is in the store. A superseded clause that outranks the current one, a duplicate that outvotes the canonical page, a regional variant that reads as a contradiction, or a poisoned rule from an unvetted source each does more damage than a missed chunk. The fixes are mostly metadata and governance (validity windows, authority tiers, version chains, ACLs, owners), enforced as hard filters and write-path controls rather than learned rankings. This changes where LLM budget at ingestion should go. Spending it on prose enrichment buys little once the retriever is strong. Spending it on structure pays far more: typing units, extracting rule cards with their cross-references, compiling procedures into step graphs, and verifying definitions against spans. The best evidence also shows that the searchable knowledge base and the enforcement layer are the same records seen from two sides. A rule card is what the agent reads, and its binding is what the gate executes, so they must share one ID, one version and one owner.

Two implications follow for anyone building this in practice. First, the durable asset is the governed unit format and its lifecycle, not the retriever or the vendor. The commercial market's churn and lock-in fall on exactly the parts that are hard to migrate: ACL mappings, verification metadata and procedure definitions. An open, portable envelope for these is worth more than another chunking strategy. Second, nobody has yet shown the full layered design beating a strong flat hybrid baseline on a heterogeneous enterprise corpus with independent measurement, and many headline results come from the systems' own authors or vendors. The first deliverable of any project should therefore be its own golden set, its applicability probes and its process-compliance simulations. Every richer layer (wiki, taxonomy, graph, runtime) should then earn its place against that baseline, one measured phase at a time.
