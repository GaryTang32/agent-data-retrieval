# Searchable Agent Knowledge Base Methods Catalog

This catalog lists the methods, systems, models, frameworks, standards and products found in the 8 research-note files on converting unstructured data into a searchable knowledge base for AI agents: domain knowledge, rules and policies, and SOPs and procedures that agents find by search, browse, read and follow, as opposed to the typed, exactly queryable database covered by the earlier *Deterministic Retrieval Methods Catalog*. For each entry it gives the developer and the type of organisation behind it, the first release date, the high-level flow, the main tools or models, and one headline result labelled by who measured it. Entries are grouped into 8 categories that follow the knowledge base from indexing to procedures, rules, domain organisation, agent access, lifecycle, commercial platforms and open-source practice. The research notes are the source for all descriptions; developers were checked against author affiliations on the first page of each paper (local PDFs or arXiv), and arXiv dates were looked up through the arXiv API. Methods already in the earlier catalog are not repeated; each category ends with a cross-reference line instead. Notes snapshot: November 2026.

## How to read this

- **Main tables vs. "Also covered briefly".** Each category has a table of the entries the notes treat in most depth, followed by a one-line list of further entries the notes describe in less detail (name, developer, first release). Benchmarks and datasets (τ-bench, τ-Knowledge, SOPBench, SOP-Bench, SkillsBench, RuleArena, HoH, CRAG, LoCoMo, MemoryAgentBench, BrowseComp-Plus, EnterpriseRAG-Bench and others) are not catalogued as methods unless they ship one; they are named in the pattern notes and family summaries where they carry the evidence.
- **"Also in the earlier catalog".** Methods that the earlier catalog already describes, and that the new notes revisit without substantially new information, are listed by name only. Where the new notes cover a different part of a system (for example GraphRAG's community reports rather than its graph builder), the new part gets its own row.
- **Developer.** Organisation(s) from the paper's affiliations or the product owner, with lead authors in parentheses where useful. "unverified" means the affiliation could not be confirmed from the source; independent researchers without an institution are filed as *Open-source community*.
- **Developer type.** One of: *LLM lab* (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta AI/FAIR, Baichuan, …), *Big tech* (Google non-DeepMind, Microsoft, Amazon, IBM, Alibaba, Tencent, Huawei, NVIDIA, Apple, Salesforce, Uber, LinkedIn, Airbnb and other large technology companies), *Data platform* (Databricks, Snowflake, Elastic), *Startup/vendor* (startups, specialist vendors, and non-tech enterprises such as banks and consultancies), *Academia* (universities, public research institutes, non-profit labs such as AI2, BAAI and Shanghai AI Lab), *Academia + industry* (with the industry partner named), *Open-source community* (community projects, independent authors and standards bodies such as W3C, OASIS, OMG and the KCS Consortium). Where a paper's first page lists Google DeepMind, it is filed as LLM lab.
- **First release.** For arXiv papers, the month of the first arXiv version (v1), taken from the arXiv API; this can differ from the month implied by the arXiv ID. Where a venue publication or general availability came before the arXiv posting, the earlier date is given with a note. For products, the first public release or announcement from the notes; "~" marks dates not verified against an official source, and "n/a" means unknown. For products the notes describe mainly through a later milestone, that milestone is shown (e.g. "n/a (docs 2026-08)").
- **High-level flow** is an arrow chain of the processing steps. **Key result** is one headline number with its provenance: *(author)* = reported by the method's own authors; *(vendor)* / *(company-reported)* = reported by the selling or deploying company; *(indep.)* = measured by a party that did not build the system; "as reported in X" = a number taken from another paper's comparison. Author-reported numbers are not comparable across papers, and most 2026 entries are unreviewed preprints.

**Pattern**

- **LLM labs own the agent-side conventions, mostly through engineering posts rather than papers.** Of 16 LLM-lab entries, the influential ones are Anthropic's Agent Skills (SKILL.md with progressive disclosure, now a Linux Foundation standard), CLAUDE.md-style context engineering, the memory tool, programmatic tool calling and prompt caching, and OpenAI's "AGENTS.md as a map" harness. Only Clio, Constitutional Classifiers and the Google DeepMind embedding and retrieval-theory papers (MUVERA, LIMIT, Gemini Embedding, EmbeddingGemma) come with formal evaluations; the skill and context-file conventions were later tested by independent groups (SkillsBench, SWE-Skills-Bench, the AGENTS.md ablations), with mixed results.
- **Microsoft is the most active big-tech developer, through IT operations and graph summarisation.** Its Microsoft Research and Azure teams produced the troubleshooting-guide line (AutoTSG, Nissist, StepFly, TSG Suggester, FixItFlow), the GraphRAG / LazyGraphRAG / OG-RAG / TnT-LLM organisation methods, and the deterministic permission-enforcement position (participant-aware access control, Azure document-level ACLs, the ISE SharePoint pattern).
- **AWS and IBM lead on enforceable rules.** AWS ships the production policy engines (Cedar, Automated Reasoning checks, AgentCore Policy); IBM Research publishes the neuro-symbolic and guard-code methods (ToolGuard, policy-as-logic, JevAct). Academia supplies most rule-extraction, deontic-logic and legal-retrieval work (16 of 35 main entries and 23 of 43 brief entries in category 3).
- **Academia dominates indexing, organisation and lifecycle research.** Academic or academia-led groups produced most entries in chunking and enrichment, taxonomy and wiki compilation, temporal retrieval, conflict handling, poisoning and RAG evaluation (about 170 of the roughly 460 catalogued entries are purely academic, and another 73 are co-developed). The most rigorous independent tests of popular methods (late chunking, RAPTOR, LumberChunker, memory vendors, llms.txt) also come from academic groups.
- **Commercial platforms converge on one design, but publish no retrieval metrics.** All 18 category-7 products come from big tech, startups or data platforms. Enterprise assistants (Glean, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Gemini Enterprise, Rovo, Moveworks) share connectors with ACL and identity sync, hybrid indexes and query-time trimming; customer-service vendors (Agentforce, Fin, Decagon, Sierra, Rasa) all pair natural-language procedures with deterministic guards and simulation tests. Effectiveness figures are vendor-reported; the UK government Copilot trials are the only independent evaluations in the notes.
- **The best production evidence comes from company engineering write-ups.** Uber, LinkedIn, Airbnb, Dropbox, Notion, Elastic, DoorDash and Shopify report that structure-preserving representations (table-aware chunks, ticket section trees, ICA decision trees), document-quality loops and calibrated LLM judges move results far more than embedding-model swaps (LinkedIn +77.6% MRR, Airbnb 0.57 → 0.92 accuracy).

## 1. Indexing, chunking and enrichment

Turning documents into retrievable units: chunking, index-side enrichment (prefixes, summaries, generated questions, metadata), granularity and multi-representation, embedding and sparse models, and navigation indexes. Independent 2025–2026 studies favour structure-led chunks with a free heading-path prefix, hybrid lexical + dense retrieval and a reranker; LLM chunkers, late chunking and LLM summary trees win only in narrow regimes. ## Method families at a glance

- **Structure-led chunking with free context prefixes** (heading-path / title-chain prefixes, SAC per-document summaries, RAGFlow Title Chunker, StrucChunk, row-tree tables). *Best for:* policies, manuals, legal codes and other documents with headings. *Evidence:* independent 2025–2026 studies on legal, regulatory and enterprise corpora favour section-aligned chunks of roughly 256–512 tokens with no overlap; LLM chunkers (LumberChunker, Meta-Chunking), late chunking and RAPTOR-style summary trees win only in narrow regimes (long narrative text, in-document search) and cost far more. Keep chunks as a re-runnable view over the parsed document tree.
- **Index-side enrichment and multiple representations per unit** (generated questions via doc2query / QuOTE / HyPE, per-document summaries, keyword and metadata tagging, propositions, ColBERT / ColPali multi-vectors, contextual chunk embedders such as voyage-context-3, pplx-embed-context and InSeNT). *Best for:* lexical retrieval, question-style agent queries and collections of near-identical documents. *Evidence:* gains shrink toward zero under strong embedders or rerankers, and enrichment shown to the reader can lower accuracy; store generated text as separate, filtered fields that point to the parent chunk, and gate each LLM step on the KB's own evaluation.
- **Hybrid lexical + dense retrieval with reranking and parent expansion** (BM25 or SPLADE, BGE-M3 / Qwen3 / EmbeddingGemma, weighted RRF in OpenSearch, Qdrant, Vespa, Elasticsearch, ParadeDB). *Best for:* every KB; lexical search is mandatory for codes, versions, policy numbers and acronyms. *Evidence:* hybrid beats either channel alone in agent loops (Airbnb 83.3 vs 78.0 / 78.8), RRF is not always the best fusion (Vespa), and reranking is the cheapest accuracy lever for search agents.
- **Procedure IR + progressive disclosure + runtime enforcement** (SKILL.md Agent Skills, SOP-Agent, FlowAgent, Compile-then-Page, StepFly, TSG Suggester, SOPRAG; commercially Decagon AOPs, Fin Procedures, Agentforce Agent Script, Rasa CALM). *Best for:* SOPs, runbooks and customer-service workflows. *Evidence:* a structured skeleton (graph or pseudo-code) with NL inside each node beats prose where prose is ambiguous; curated, compact procedures help (+16.6 pp on SkillsBench) while self-generated or over-prescriptive ones hurt; compliance gains come from controllers, state machines and simulation tests rather than from the representation alone.
- **Dual-representation rules: rule cards plus executable bindings** (De Jure, RegReAct, ComplianceNLP for extraction; Cedar, Rego, Catala, ASP, SMT, DMN for execution; ToolGuard, Automated Reasoning checks, AgentCore Policy, Agent-C for enforcement; LegalRuleML, Akoma Ntoso, ODRL for interchange). *Best for:* regulations and business policies that gate actions or compute numbers. *Evidence:* LLM fact extraction + symbolic evaluation beats both policy-as-prompt and LLM-written policy code on crisp rules (RuleArena Airline up to 1.00 vs ≤0.38) but not on subjective ones; temporal validity must be a hard filter; compile rates overstate correctness (95% compile vs 50% end-to-end correct).
- **Navigation and synthesized knowledge layers** (corpus-grounded taxonomies via Clio, TnT-LLM, TaxoAdapt, Corpus2Skill; SKOS glossaries and alias tables; LLM wikis per Karpathy, LLM-Wiki, RAGFlow compilation; GraphRAG community reports, LazyGraphRAG, HippoRAG). *Best for:* bounded, topical corpora and "what is related / what are the themes" questions. *Evidence:* navigation won 5 of 11 datasets in Corpus2Skill and lost on open-domain and tabular corpora; under a strong agentic harness compiled wikis and graphs were accuracy-neutral but cut tokens by 38%; generated pages need claim + verbatim-evidence structure and faithfulness checks (STORM ~15% unsupported sentences).
- **Small agent tool surface over files and indexes** (search / read / grep / related tools, compact always-loaded index or AGENTS.md map, Dr-DCI-style retrieval-scoped workspaces, bounded loops with a first-move planner, RL-trained search agents such as Search-R1 and KARL). *Best for:* agents that must find, read and cite KB content. *Evidence:* two retrieval rounds capture ~95% of loop gains, one level of progressive disclosure helps while deeper hierarchies hurt, always-loaded instruction files are obeyed even when stale, and citation validity should be enforced in code. Agent memory works best as provenance-tracked write-back consolidated into procedures (AWM, ACE, Memp); commercial memory layers often trail BM25 on independent benchmarks.
